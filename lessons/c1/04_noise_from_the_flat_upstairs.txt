Noise from the Flat Upstairs
A: Hi, sorry to bother you—I live in the flat right below yours. Honestly, I was reluctant to knock, because I know a new kitchen can't go in quietly.
B: Oh, no, I'm so sorry! The noise has been awful all morning, hasn't it? Don't look past me—the whole interior is in complete chaos right now.
A: Don't worry about the mess. Normally I just shut my study door and cope with it, except I have an online job interview from two to three today.
B: I'm really glad you came up! I meant to notify all the residents in the building, and in the rush I only taped a note by the lift.
A: Ah, I'm on the first floor, so I always take the stairs. Could the workers pause the heavy work for that one hour?
B: The only problem is they've hired the heavy equipment just for today, so they have to finish the concrete wall before five.
A: Could we find a compromise, then? I don't mind how loud it gets before half past one or straight after three, as long as it's quiet in between.
B: That works! They can take lunch at half past one and put the cupboard doors on after two—that'll minimize the disruption during your call, and they'll still finish on time.
A: That really saves me, thank you.
B: Actually, while you're here, the worker who's fixing the pipes needs to turn the water off for an hour tomorrow morning to replace an old kitchen drain. Would you have any objection if he does it at ten?
A: Ten is fine, as long as I know in advance! You can imagine my frustration last year when someone shut the water off while I had soap in my hair.
B: What a nightmare! I'll put a proper note under everyone's door tonight—and once the dust settles next week, come up for tea so you can see what all the noise was for.

Vocabulary: chaos, compromise, concrete, cope, disruption, drain, frustration, interior, minimize, notify, objection, reluctant, resident
