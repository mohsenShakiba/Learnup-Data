Arranging a Nursery Collection
A: Before I take Ella home, could I ask about tomorrow? My sister needs to collect her from nursery because I'll be working late.
B: Certainly. Is your sister already on Ella's list of approved contacts?
A: No, she's never collected her on my behalf before. Can I authorize her now?
B: Yes. We need written parental consent, so please complete this form with her full name and phone number.
A: I thought verbal consent would be enough, since I'm here in person.
B: We need a record that the afternoon staff can check. For tomorrow's collection, the signed form is enough; for a regular arrangement, we would also update Ella's contact list.
A: Just tomorrow for now. What documentation will my sister need to bring?
B: Photo identification, such as a passport. The name must match the one you put on the form.
A: She got married recently, so I'll check which name is on her passport.
B: Thank you. We can't rely solely on a child recognizing someone, even when they're a relative.
A: That makes sense. As for the time, she may arrive a little after five. Should I put that here too?
B: Yes, please. And could you ask her to come to the front desk first? We'll check her details before bringing Ella out.
A: Of course. What if her train is delayed and my brother has to come instead?
B: In that case, contact us before he arrives. You'd need to complete another form naming him as the person collecting Ella.
A: Understood. I don't want anyone arriving here and finding they can't take her home.
B: Exactly. This isn't something we leave to individual staff members' discretion. That said, we can help you complete the form through the app if plans change while you're at work.
A: That's useful. I'll call my sister now to check her full name, then bring this back before we leave.
B: Thank you. I'll make sure the afternoon team has the details.

Vocabulary: authorize, behalf, discretion, documentation, identification, nursery, parental, solely, verbal
