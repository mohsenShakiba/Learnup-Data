A Rumour About the School
A: Could you pass me the school letter from the kitchen table? There's a message in the parents' group saying the school is closing permanently.
B: Here. Someone sent it to me too. The rumour seems to have started with an anonymous post last night.
A: It says the children are moving to another school in January. That's what this letter says as well.
B: For six weeks while the heating is replaced. The post leaves that part out, which distorts the meaning quite a bit.
A: You're right. I read the message first and assumed the letter would say the same thing. Has the school put out a denial?
B: Not that I've seen. I'm sceptical about the post, though. The writer claims to work at the school but won't say what they do there.
A: Maybe they're afraid of losing their job. Being anonymous doesn't automatically mean they're wrong.
B: No, it doesn't. But it doesn't give the story credibility either. Accusing the head teacher of hiding a decision is a serious allegation to make without any evidence.
A: There's a photo of a letter below the post. Presumably that's where the story came from.
B: Possibly, but there's no date or school name on it. We can't even tell which school it's about.
A: And the post is vague about where it came from. I was about to send all this to my sister; her son starts there next year.
B: I'd wait. The same post can get passed around until it looks like several different reports.
A: I don't want to dismiss it if there really is a problem, though.
B: Neither do I. Let's ask the school directly whether there's any plan to close permanently, and attach the post so they know what we're referring to.
A: I'll do that. For now, I'll put the full letter in the group and point out that it says six weeks.
B: Good idea. We can correct that part without pretending we know everything else.

Vocabulary: allegation, anonymous, credibility, denial, distort, presumably, rumour, sceptical, vague
