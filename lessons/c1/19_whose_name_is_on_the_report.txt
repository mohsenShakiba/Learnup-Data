Whose Name Is on the Report?
A: Before you send the report on the queues to head office, can we talk about the names on it? I saw the draft on the printer.
B: Sure. What about them?
A: Mine was on the first page yesterday. Now it's gone.
B: Oh, I used the form they sent this morning for the first page. It only asks for one contact, so I put myself down.
A: I don't mind you being the contact. It's this line on the next page. "I conducted interviews with staff at all three shops." I did those interviews.
B: You're right. I was working on the assumption that the report could only have one name on it too. I changed the summary to match.
A: I see. The way it reads now implies you did the whole thing. The proposal to put returns through a separate till came out of those interviews.
B: It did. What if we add a line to acknowledge your contribution?
A: Could we put both names on the report itself? My review's next week, and I'd like some recognition for the work I've done.
B: Of course. I'll put your name back on the report and change that line to say you did the interviews.
A: Thanks. And if they want a meeting about how to implement it, can I come? I'd like to suggest trying it in just one shop first.
B: Yes. I'll ask them to include you when I send it over. Do you want another look before I submit it?
A: Please. I'll read it as soon as it comes through.

Vocabulary: acknowledge, assumption, conduct, contribution, draft, implement, imply, proposal, recognition, submit
