The Bridge Closure Plan
A: Before we head into the council chamber at six, did you get a chance to read the report from Tuesday's public consultation?
B: Yes, I went through it on the train. Turnout at the town hall was much higher than I expected, especially with that petition from two hundred local businesses.
A: Every seat was taken. On the whole, the closure of the old river bridge to general traffic had strong support from residents.
B: Right—most of the objections came from the market shops and cafes over the all-day ban on delivery vehicles.
A: Honestly, I understood their frustration on the night. The lack of clarity in our first draft about morning deliveries made things much worse.
B: Have the transport team found a compromise before tonight's vote?
A: We drafted a short amendment this afternoon. It lets smaller delivery vehicles cross the bridge until nine in the morning, before the square gets busy.
B: That sounds sensible. It should minimize disruption for the shops, and it still keeps heavy trucks off the bridge all day.
A: Do you think the other councillors will back the change at this stage?
B: If we explain it clearly, yes. Otherwise, the plan will face strong resistance from businesses on day one.
A: True. Shall I print twenty copies of the revised text so everyone at the table can read it?
B: Yes, thanks—and I'll mention it to the chair before the meeting starts.

Vocabulary: amendment, chamber, clarity, closure, compromise, consultation, councillor, disruption, frustration, minimize, objection, petition, resistance, turnout
