The Bridge Closure Plan
A: Before we go into the council chamber at six, did you get a chance to read the report from Tuesday's consultation on the bridge closure?
B: Yes, on the train. I knew two hundred local businesses had signed the petition, but I wasn't expecting quite that turnout.
A: Every seat was taken. Most of the residents who spoke wanted the old bridge closed to general traffic.
B: The shop and cafe owners had a fair objection, though. How are they supposed to get their deliveries if vans can't cross all day?
A: I can understand their frustration. Our first draft didn't even make it clear whether morning deliveries could get through.
B: Have the transport team come up with a compromise for tonight's vote?
A: We drafted a short amendment this afternoon. It lets smaller delivery vehicles cross the bridge until nine in the morning, before the square gets busy.
B: That sounds sensible. It should minimize disruption for the shops, and it still keeps heavy trucks off the bridge all day.
A: Do you think the other councillors will back the change at this stage?
B: I think so. We'll face a lot less resistance from the shops if they can still get their deliveries.
A: True. Shall I print twenty copies of the revised text so everyone at the table can read it?
B: Yes, thanks—and I'll mention it to the chair before the meeting starts.

Vocabulary: amendment, chamber, closure, compromise, consultation, councillor, disruption, frustration, minimize, objection, petition, resistance, turnout
