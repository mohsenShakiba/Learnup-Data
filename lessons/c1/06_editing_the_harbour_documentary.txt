Editing the Harbour Documentary
A: How's the rough cut of our harbour documentary coming along in here? You've been staring at that studio screen since nine.
B: I'm nearly there. What's slowing me down is the low noise during our cafe interview with the retired captain.
A: Was the wind hitting the window again?
B: No, it wasn't the wind outside that caused it—it was that old fridge running behind the counter. I've put an audio filter on the track, and it sounds much cleaner now.
A: Let me listen... Oh, that's miles better. Reading through the full transcript last night, I'd forgotten how moving his story was.
B: I felt the same way. What surprised me most was how open he became when he talked about the fishing fleet closing down.
A: Actually, it was your quiet question about his father's boat that really got him talking. It keeps the whole scene feeling authentic.
B: Do you think we still need any spoken commentary over the opening harbour footage, then?
A: Honestly, I don't think we need any. His own words are far more compelling, especially over a brief glimpse of the old black-and-white photos from the town archive.
B: Speaking of those photos, did the museum send back the copyright permission and the captain's signed consent form?
A: Both arrived by email an hour ago, so we're clear to use everything.
B: That's great news. What we need to finish today is a two-minute trailer that portrays the town fairly without giving the whole story away.
A: Grab a coffee while I pull the best three scenes together, and we can watch it back before five.
