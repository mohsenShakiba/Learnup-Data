Editing the Harbour Documentary
A: Play that ending again in a minute—I honestly didn't expect a two-minute trailer to move me like that.
B: Really? What I was worried about was the sound in the old captain's kitchen. Before I put a filter on his audio track, his fridge was louder than he was.
A: You'd never guess now. I typed up the interview transcript myself last week, yet it's that quiet pause before he says his dad's name that really gets you.
B: Me too. Do you think people watching the film will need a line of spoken commentary over the harbour shot, though? You only catch a brief glimpse of his dad's boat in our footage from that wet morning, so they might not realize which one he's pointing at.
A: Rather than talking over him, what if we cut to that black-and-white photo I found in the town archive—the one of him at sixteen right beside his dad, with the whole fishing fleet behind them?
B: Oh, that's a much better idea—his own words do all the work then. Who holds the copyright on that photo, though?
A: The local museum does. When I copied it last week, they said local projects can use archive photos for free as long as we thank the museum at the end.
B: Even better. And did the captain ever sign his consent form? I don't want to put the trailer online on Friday until all the forms are back.
A: It's right here in my bag—I walked down to the harbour to collect it from him, and he made me stay for two cups of tea and three more stories.
B: That explains why you were gone all afternoon! Send me a copy of that photo, then, and let's try it in the cut.
