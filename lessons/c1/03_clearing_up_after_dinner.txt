Clearing Up After Dinner
A: You can put those glasses on the counter. Where did all the dinner plates go?
B: Clara washed most of them. She wandered in while I was making tea. I haven't laughed that much in ages.
A: Really? She was so quiet at dinner. Every time I glanced over, she was just nodding along to one of Dad's stories.
B: Well, even I lost the thread of that story about Pete and the sofa, and I helped carry the thing up the stairs.
A: She asked me who Pete was. I said, "The tall one." She gave me this little grin, and it took me a minute. Pete wasn't even here.
B: She did get the bit about the sofa. Her dad took a door off to get theirs out of the house. Then her mum walked in and took the legs off the sofa.
A: Dad would love that. I wish he'd heard her tell it.
B: He nearly did. She started to tell him at the table, but you were both trying to remember which floor Pete lived on.
A: Oh. I remember feeling like an outsider at my first dinner with your family. It was a relief when you asked me to help with the dishes.
B: I reckon Mum still thinks you love washing up. She told everyone how thoughtful you were.
A: I only offered to wash because you said you'd dry. I'd been there two hours and I'd barely had a word with you.

Vocabulary: counter, glance, grin, nod, outsider, reckon, relief, thoughtful, thread, wander
