Renewing the Rental Agreement
A: Did you get a letter from our landlord in the post today too?
B: Yes, I've just opened mine down here in the lobby. Is yours the new rental agreement for next year as well?
A: It is. I was hoping to renew when my contract expires next month, but that new monthly maintenance charge took me by surprise.
B: Me too. It's pretty misleading to call it a minor update at the top when it adds eighty pounds a month.
A: Frankly, the explanation he gives is completely inadequate—the heating in here barely worked all winter, and the lift still stops between floors.
B: Exactly. Moving somewhere else is costly right now, and he probably thinks we'll just pay it.
A: Well, I'm not signing it like this. Look at page two—he's included a garden fee too, when we don't even get a key to the garden.
B: Oh, good catch. At least with that one, he must have used a general form and overlooked that section.
A: Should we write back together and ask for a full breakdown of the figures before we sign anything?
B: Definitely. Once the other tenants get home from work, we can ask if they want to add their names too.
A: Good idea. I'll type up a short email tonight and send it to you to check first.
B: Thanks. Knock on my door if you want a hand with it.

Vocabulary: breakdown, costly, expire, frankly, inadequate, landlord, lobby, maintenance, misleading, overlook, renew, rental, tenant
