Trying Out the Local Choir
A: You're smiling, and you've come home with a blue folder! I take it your first evening with the local choir went well.
B: Better than I expected, actually—though I have to confess I hesitated outside the door for a minute before I went in.
A: Why? Had they already started singing when you got to the school on Park Road?
B: No, people were still putting the chairs out in the main hall. I suddenly realized I hadn't sung in front of anyone since I was at school.
A: Did you know anybody there?
B: Not at first, which didn't help my nerves. Fortunately, the woman who leads the group spotted me by the door and came over to reassure me.
A: Did she make you sing on your own to see how high you could go?
B: No, I'm glad to say! It's an amateur group, and she never asks a new member to sing a solo.
A: How did you manage with the sheet music? I know you were worried about following the notes.
B: She sat me next to a friendly woman who told me to ignore the notes at first and just listen for the main melody.
A: Was it easier once everyone started singing?
B: Much easier. Mistakes don't stand out when forty people are singing together, and when the back row added the harmony, it sounded terrific.
A: What's inside the blue folder?
B: She gave me the lyrics for the four songs they're learning for their upcoming winter concert.
A: Don't tell me you're singing on stage next month already!
B: Only if I feel ready by December. Nobody pushes you to take part, though the enthusiasm in that room makes it hard to say no.
A: If you decide to sing in it, save me a seat near the front so I can applaud louder than anyone else.
B: I will—just promise you won't wave at me while I'm trying to remember the words! Put the water on for tea and I'll show you what we sang.

Vocabulary: amateur, applaud, choir, confess, enthusiasm, harmony, hesitate, lyric, melody, reassure, solo, terrific, upcoming
