Checking the Fragile Orders
A: Walking into the loading bay just now, I noticed six large boxes sitting near the open door in the rain.
B: Oh no—those are the bulk orders going up to Glasgow with the four o'clock driver. Did the rain reach them?
A: Only the outside got a bit wet, thankfully—though there are empty boxes left right across the side exit, which is a real trip hazard.
B: I'll clear those out of the way in a minute. The temporary staff were rushing to finish the shipping labels before their break, and I was on the phone at the desk instead of overseeing the bay.
A: Look—that top box marked fragile is crushed at one end, too. Should we inspect the bottles inside before the truck arrives?
B: Definitely. Given what happened with those broken bottles last month, I don't want to take any chances today.
A: Pass me the knife and I'll open the tape... That's good news—every bottle inside is completely intact.
B: Hang on, look at the top seal on this bottle on the left. It's slightly bent at the edge.
A: You're right, that's a factory defect, not damage from packing. If oil leaks out, it'll ruin the whole box.
B: Let's set that bottle aside and bring a replacement from the main warehouse.
A: I'll wrap an extra sheet of thick paper around each bottle too, so nothing moves in transit on the long drive north.
B: Thanks. Once we tape this box shut again, let's move the whole pile further inside the bay until the driver pulls up.

Vocabulary: bay, bulk, crush, defect, fragile, hazard, inspect, intact, leak, oversee, replacement, seal, shipping, thankfully, transit, warehouse
