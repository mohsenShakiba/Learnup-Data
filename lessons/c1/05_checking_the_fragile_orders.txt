Checking the Fragile Orders
A: There are six big boxes getting wet by the open loading bay door.
B: Oh no—those are the bulk orders going up to Glasgow with the four o'clock driver. How wet are they?
A: Just a little wet on the outside, thankfully. Mind those empty boxes by the side exit, though. They're a trip hazard.
B: I'll shift the empty ones. Can you help me get the Glasgow boxes further inside?
A: Sure. Hang on, this top box marked fragile is crushed at one end. Should we inspect the bottles before the truck gets here?
B: Definitely. Given what happened with those broken bottles last month, I don't want to take any chances today.
A: Pass me the knife and I'll cut the tape... Well, the bottles look intact.
B: They do, but look at the seal on this one. See how the edge is bent?
A: Oh, I see it. Looks like a factory defect. If the oil leaks out, it'll ruin the whole box.
B: Let's set that bottle aside and bring a replacement from the main warehouse.
A: I'll wrap an extra sheet of thick paper around each bottle too, so nothing moves in transit on the long drive north.
B: Thanks. I'll put a fresh shipping label on it once we've taped it up.

Vocabulary: bay, bulk, crush, defect, fragile, hazard, inspect, intact, leak, replacement, seal, shipping, thankfully, transit, warehouse
