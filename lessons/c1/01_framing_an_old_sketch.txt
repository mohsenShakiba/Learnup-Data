Framing an Old Sketch
A: What did you bring home in that brown paper?
B: Come over to the kitchen table while I take the paper off. I walked past the market stalls on my way back and found something for that bare wall by the stairs.
A: Oh, it's a pencil sketch of the old canal! Look at the little boats that used to stop near the bridge.
B: That's why I liked it. It was only twelve pounds with the wooden frame, so I thought it was a real bargain.
A: Definitely. There's a lot of dust under the glass, though, and the paper has started to fade along the top edge.
B: Yes, the woman on the stall said it used to hang opposite a bright window. Still, if I open the back and wipe the inside of the glass, it'll look much cleaner.
A: Just be careful if you take the glass out. That old paper looks very delicate, and you could easily rip the edge if it slips.
B: Good point. Hang on—look down here at the bottom right, just where the card has slipped.
A: Is that a name and a date in pencil? It says May 1962!
B: Really? Let me see. I assumed it was just a cheap copy when I bought it, not a genuine drawing.
A: In that case, we shouldn't try taking the frame apart ourselves. Why don't we take it to the frame shop by the station instead?
B: Do you think they'll charge a lot? I don't want a twelve-pound picture to turn into a costly job.
A: Cleaning the glass and putting a new back on shouldn't cost much. We could also ask how much they charge for protective glass, since the stairs get so much sun in the afternoon.
B: That'll be worthwhile if it stops the pencil lines from fading any further. Are they open on Saturday afternoons?
A: Until half past four, as far as I remember. If we walk down there after a cup of tea, we'll get there before they close.
B: Great. Leave the brown paper on the table so I can wrap it up safely again before we go.

Vocabulary: bare, bargain, canal, costly, delicate, fade, genuine, protective, rip, sketch, stall, wipe, worthwhile
