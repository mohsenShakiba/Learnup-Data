Choosing a Nursing Course
A: I'm interested in nursing, but I'm not sure which course to apply for. Could you help me compare these two?
B: Certainly. Are you hoping to study here, or would you move away?
A: I'd prefer this institute because I could stay at home. The other course is in another city.
B: That could make a big difference to the cost. Have you looked at the funding available for each one?
A: The other college offers a scholarship, but only to a few students. I can't plan on getting it.
B: Let's compare what you'd learn, then. Have you had any experience of nursing?
A: I've been volunteering in healthcare for six months. I'm passionate about helping patients, but I know that isn't enough on its own.
B: What have you learned from the nurses there?
A: How much they need to know, and how carefully they check things. I'd like to specialize in working with children eventually.
B: Then look at the practical experience each course offers. This one includes a placement at the local children's hospital.
A: That's useful. Does every student go there?
B: The description doesn't say. We should ask before you make that part of your decision.
A: The other course has more psychology in the first year. I'd like to understand the psychological effects of a long illness.
B: You could ask how that subject is taught here too. The course titles don't tell us everything.
A: I'll write down both questions. Is there an open day soon?
B: Next Thursday. You can meet the teaching staff and ask current students about their experience.

Vocabulary: funding, healthcare, institute, nursing, passionate, placement, psychological, psychology, scholarship, specialize
