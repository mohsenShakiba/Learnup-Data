Choosing a Nursing Course
A: Is this the office for careers advice? I'd like some help choosing a college course.
B: Yes. Come in and take a seat. What would you like to study?
A: Nursing. I've brought the details of two courses, but I'm not sure which one to apply for.
B: Let's have a look. Is there one you prefer so far?
A: The college here in town would be much easier because I could stay at home. The other course is in another city.
B: Staying at home would help with the cost. Have you looked into funding for either course?
A: A few students get their fees paid, but I couldn't count on that. I'd have to keep my weekend job.
B: No, so it's worth comparing the costs without it. What else matters to you about the course?
A: Practical experience. I've been volunteering at the hospital for six months. The nurses there have inspired me to work in healthcare.
B: Is there a part of nursing that particularly interests you?
A: Yes, cancer care. My mum had breast cancer when I was little. One nurse always made time for my questions. That's why I'm so passionate about working with families.
B: Then look at this course. Students spend a month on a hospital team that supports patients and their families.
A: Could I talk to someone on that team? I'm interested in what happens after treatment too. Mum's a survivor, and she still goes back once a year.
B: The course page doesn't name anyone, but the college could put you in touch. Are you going to the open day?
A: Yes, on Thursday. I'll ask then. I'd also like to know how students deal with the emotional side of the job. It must be challenging.
B: It is. Talk to the students who have already spent time on that team. They'll know what support they had when they needed it.
A: Good idea. I'll write those questions down and take them with me.

Vocabulary: breast, cancer, challenging, emotional, funding, healthcare, inspire, nursing, passionate, survivor
