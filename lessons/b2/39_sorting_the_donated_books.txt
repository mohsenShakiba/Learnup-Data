Sorting the Donated Books
A: Are these the boxes of books that the retired teacher brought into the shop this morning?
B: Yes. He taught at the university for forty years, so most are about history and literature. There are a few fantasy and romance novels too.
A: How should we sort them? We don't have a separate section for university books.
B: Put the books about history and literature by the window. The contemporary novels can go on the front table with the other recent books.
A: What about these older novels at the bottom of the box?
B: Let me see. Most of these are well-known classics that students buy every autumn, so price them at three pounds each.
A: Wait—look at this one. It's from 1910, and there are illustrations on almost every page.
B: Careful with that page. There's a letter inside, and it's signed by the novelist himself.
A: Do you think the teacher meant to leave that letter in the book?
B: I don't know. We should call him before we sell it. A collector might be interested in the letter, but it might mean something to his family too.
A: If he meant to donate it, could we sell it at auction?
B: Perhaps. We'd need to ask a specialist book dealer to value it first. Its age alone doesn't tell us what it's worth.
A: I'll put it in the office safe for now. Do we have the teacher's phone number?
B: Yes, he left it on the delivery note in case we wanted to collect his remaining boxes. I'll call him now.

Vocabulary: auction, classic, collector, contemporary, dealer, fantasy, illustration, novelist, romance, specialist
