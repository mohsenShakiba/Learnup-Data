Sorting the Donated Books
A: Are these the boxes of books that the retired teacher brought into the shop this morning?
B: Yes. He taught at the university for forty years, so most are about history and literature. There are a few fantasy and romance novels too.
A: How should we sort them? We don't have a separate section for university books.
B: Put the history books by the window. Anything from the last ten years can go on the front table, apart from children's books. There's a separate shelf for those.
A: What about these older novels at the bottom of the box?
B: Let me see. Most of these are well-known classics that students buy every autumn, so price them at three pounds each.
A: Wait, look at this one. It's a comic from 1910 about a boy and a monster. There's a loose sheet of paper inside, with a little pencil drawing on it.
B: Oh, it's the boy's face. See what it says below the picture? "My first creation." I know that signature. It's the man who drew the comic.
A: Do you think the teacher knew that drawing was in the book?
B: Maybe not. We should call him. A collector might pay good money for a drawing like that, but it might mean something to him too.
A: If he meant to give it to us, how would we price it? Is there a guide to the value of old comics?
B: Our specialist book dealer might know. Her family worked in publishing for years. She can tell us whether people still buy this one.
A: I'll put it in the office safe for now. Do we have the teacher's phone number?
B: Yes, he left it on the delivery note in case we wanted to collect his remaining boxes. I'll call him now.

Vocabulary: classic, collector, comic, creation, dealer, fantasy, monster, publishing, romance, specialist
