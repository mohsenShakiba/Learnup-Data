Sorting the Donated Books
A: Are these the boxes of books that the retired scholar brought into the shop this morning?
B: Yes. He taught at the university for forty years, so there are dozens of literary studies and histories in here.
A: How should we classify them for the shelves? We don't have a separate section for university books.
B: Put the histories by the window, and keep the contemporary stories for the front table. People always look for recent books first.
A: What about these older novels at the bottom of the box?
B: Let me see. Most of these are well-known classics that students buy every autumn, so price them at three pounds each.
A: Wait—look at this one. It's from 1910, and it has painted illustrations on thick paper.
B: Careful with that page. There's a letter inside, and it's signed by the novelist himself.
A: Do you think the scholar knew he was giving away something so precious?
B: We must call him and check before we do anything with it. A book collector would pay a lot for a signed copy with a letter.
A: It could be worth a small fortune at an auction.
B: If he meant to give it to the shop, we shouldn't just put it on the shelf. We should ask a specialist book dealer to value it properly.
A: I'll put it in the office safe for now. Do we have his phone number?
B: Yes, he left it on the delivery note in case we wanted to collect his remaining boxes. I'll call him now.

Vocabulary: auction, classic, classify, collector, contemporary, dealer, fortune, illustration, literary, novelist, precious, scholar, specialist
