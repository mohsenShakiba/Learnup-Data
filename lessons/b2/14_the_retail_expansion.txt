The Retail Expansion
A: Have you read the proposal from the investor who visited our cafe on Monday?
B: Yes, I went through the numbers last night. He wants to form a partnership with us and open a second shop downtown.
A: It sounds exciting. A central shop could really boost our sales.
B: Maybe, but higher revenue doesn't always mean more profit. Our accountant checked the figures, and the rent on that street is huge.
A: Couldn't we use the investor's money to finance the new equipment and the shop signs?
B: Only part of it. If we signed his agreement, we'd still have to take out a large loan and carry that debt for ten years.
A: That does sound risky, especially when our profit margin on cakes is already quite narrow.
B: Exactly. And if we opened a second place now, our current kitchen wouldn't have the capacity to bake enough cakes for both shops.
A: What if we stayed in this building and planned a smaller expansion instead?
B: That's what I was thinking. The empty store room next door is available to rent. Our savings could pay the extra rent while we set it up.
A: Would it give us enough space for a bigger kitchen?
B: We need to measure it. Let's arrange to look inside, then get some prices for the work.

Vocabulary: accountant, boost, capacity, debt, downtown, expansion, finance, investor, margin, partnership, proposal, revenue, risky, saving
