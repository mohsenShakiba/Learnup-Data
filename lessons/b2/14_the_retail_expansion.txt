The Retail Expansion
A: Have you read the new proposal from the retail investor who visited our cafe on Monday?
B: Yes, I went through the numbers last night. He wants to form a partnership with us and open a second shop downtown.
A: It sounds exciting. A central shop would boost our daily sales and bring in much higher revenue.
B: Maybe, but our accountant, who checked our books yesterday, warned me that the rent on that street is huge.
A: Couldn't we use the investor's money to finance the new equipment and the shop signs?
B: Only part of it. If we signed his agreement, we'd still have to take out a large loan and carry that debt for ten years.
A: That does sound risky, especially when our profit margin on cakes is already quite narrow.
B: Exactly. And if we opened a second place now, our current kitchen wouldn't have the capacity to bake enough cakes for both shops.
A: What if we stayed in this building and planned a smaller expansion instead?
B: That's what I was thinking. If we used our own savings as a long-term investment, we could acquire the empty store room next door.
A: Good idea! The city council is even offering a tax incentive to small companies that improve their own buildings.
B: Then we wouldn't need a new mortgage, and our main asset would stay completely in our own hands.

Vocabulary: accountant, acquire, asset, boost, capacity, debt, downtown, expansion, finance, incentive, investment, investor, long-term, margin, mortgage, partnership, proposal, retail, revenue, risky, saving
