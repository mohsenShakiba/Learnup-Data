Discussing the Staff Report
A: Has the external analyst finished the report on our annual staff questions?
B: Yes, she sent it over an hour ago. Nearly eighty in every hundred members of the workforce filled it in, so it's a very comprehensive set of results.
A: Was it a good decision to ask an outside company to run it?
B: Definitely. Staff knew their answers wouldn't have their names on them, which reduced the risk of bias in the report.
A: What's the main indication from the scores? Are people happy with the flexible working hours?
B: That part scored well, and it gives us a clear insight into why fewer people have taken sick days this year.
A: What were they most critical about, then?
B: Career progress. It's evident from the comments that many junior staff don't see how to move into senior positions here.
A: Do they perceive that we always hire managers from outside the company?
B: Yes, because our last three recruitment notices went straight onto public job websites before we spoke to our own teams.
A: That's a fair complaint. Offering training for internal candidates would be a strong incentive to stay with us.
B: Exactly. Paying for a course costs much less than losing good people whose motivation has dropped.
A: Let's ask the team managers to advertise every new post internally for a week first.
B: Good idea. I'll add that proposal to the summary before we present the report on Thursday.

Vocabulary: analyst, bias, comprehensive, critical, evident, incentive, indication, insight, motivation, perceive, recruitment, workforce
