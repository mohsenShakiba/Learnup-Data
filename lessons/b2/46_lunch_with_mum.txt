Lunch with Mum
A: I'm calling about our table for six on Sunday at half past twelve, under Harris. My mum still needs a stick after her operation. Can she get in without using the steps?
B: Yes. There's a side entrance with a flat path from the car park. It's the entrance marked "Disabled Access" on the map.
A: Good. Steps are still difficult for her. Can we stop the car close to that entrance?
B: Certainly. You can drop her off there before you park. Will she need any assistance getting to the table?
A: My brother will be with her. She's keen to do things herself, though—she hates feeling dependent on us.
B: Understood. I'll give you a table near that door, with plenty of room around it.
A: Is that part of the restaurant warm? She feels the cold, so she'd be more comfortable a little further inside.
B: The door closes automatically. Table four is further inside, beside the window. That should suit your group nicely.
A: Lovely. My elderly uncle is coming too, so comfort matters more than the view. Are the chairs so low that it's hard to get up?
B: They're quite high, and the seats are firm. Is there anything else you'd like to know?
A: What about the bathroom—are there any steps on the way?
B: None at all. This whole floor is entirely flat, and the bathroom door's light, with a handle that's easy to turn.
A: That sounds good. Could you send a copy of the menu too? My uncle's blind and he can listen to it on his phone. That bit of independence is precious to him.
B: Of course. I'll email the menu with your booking details and put your name down for table four.
A: Thanks. I'll tell my brother to drive to the side door.
B: We'll see you then. I'll have the table ready before you arrive.

Vocabulary: assistance, blind, booking, comfort, dependent, disabled, elderly, entirely, handle, precious
