The First Month of Retirement
A: How's retirement treating you? You've been at home for a month now, haven't you?
B: Yes. At first, the freedom was wonderful, but lately the days have started to feel very long.
A: Do you miss the work, or the people?
B: Mostly the people. It's weird to spend the entire day without talking to anyone.
A: You used to make things out of wood. Have you had more time for that?
B: I made a little wooden box last week. Finishing it gave me more satisfaction than anything else I've done this month.
A: There's a group at the community hall. My neighbour made a walking stick there with a lovely curved handle. They meet on Tuesday mornings.
B: I've seen their sign. I liked the photo of those old chairs they'd fixed. They looked like they still had a bit of soul, even with new seats.
A: That's the idea. Everyone brings their own project and they help each other. Would you like me to introduce you to my neighbour?
B: Yes, thanks. I'd enjoy that, as long as I don't have to make things to sell. Everyone seems to think that's the ultimate goal of any hobby.
A: You don't have to sell anything. You can just make things for the joy of it.
B: Exactly. I didn't stop working just to start collecting orders again. I'd like to master something I can't do yet.
A: One morning a week could be enough. It would give you some company while you work on your next box.
B: And a reason to leave the house. Have you seen the one I finished?
A: No. Is it here?
B: On the shelf. Come and look. See how the top sticks up at the sides? I intended it to sit flat. Whoever made that walking stick could probably help.

Vocabulary: curved, freedom, joy, lately, master, retirement, satisfaction, soul, ultimate, weird
