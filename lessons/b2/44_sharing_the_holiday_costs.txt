Sharing the Holiday Costs
A: Have you worked out how much I owe you for the holiday house?
B: A hundred and eighty pounds each. There were four of us, and the overall cost of the house was seven hundred and twenty.
A: I'm not sure an equal division is fair. I stayed for two nights, whereas you three stayed for four.
B: Fair point. You should pay a smaller proportion. I just divided the total by four without thinking.
A: I don't mind paying something for the nights I missed. You booked the house before my circumstances changed.
B: We chose that house because all four of us were coming. But the three of us did have the extra space after you left.
A: What contribution would seem fair, then?
B: How about a hundred and twenty from you, and two hundred each from the rest of us?
A: I agree with that in principle, but we should ask them too. I don't want to decide what they pay.
B: Of course. I'll suggest those amounts in the group. We should resolve this before anyone sends money.
A: Thanks. For the food, I kept the receipts from the two days I was there.
B: Good. We can use the actual costs for each day and share those between the people who ate together.
A: I'll send you those. Did anyone else buy food while I was there?
B: Yes, Sam bought breakfast. I'll ask for her receipts too, so we include everything.

Vocabulary: actual, circumstance, contribution, division, overall, owe, principle, proportion, resolve, whereas
