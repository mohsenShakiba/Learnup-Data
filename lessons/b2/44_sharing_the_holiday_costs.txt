Sharing the Holiday Costs
A: Have you worked out how much I owe you for the holiday house?
B: A hundred and eighty pounds each. There were four of us, and the overall cost of the house was seven hundred and twenty.
A: Wait, does splitting it four ways make sense? I only stayed two nights. The three of you stayed all four.
B: Oh, fair point. I just divided the total by four without thinking.
A: I still want to pay something for the nights I missed. You booked it before that unexpected work trip came up.
B: It was a fabulous house—such a pity you had to leave early. And the three of us did get all the extra space after you went.
A: We'll have to go back sometime when I can stay all week. What would you suggest for this trip?
B: How about a hundred and twenty from you, and two hundred each from the rest of us? The timing was unfortunate. It doesn't seem fair to ask you for the full amount.
A: That sounds fair to me. Let's ask the others before we agree, though. What about the opera tickets Jules bought for our last night there? Did you manage to sell mine?
B: Yes, to a friend who was staying nearby. I put a message on the opera group's page and got an instant reply. You don't owe anything for that.
A: Thanks. For the food, I kept the receipts from the two days I was there.
B: Good. We can use the actual costs for each day and split those between the people who ate together.
A: I'll send you photos of mine. Did anyone else buy food while I was there?
B: Yes, Sam bought breakfast. I'll ask for her receipts too, so we include everything.

Vocabulary: actual, fabulous, instant, opera, overall, owe, pity, sometime, timing, unfortunate
