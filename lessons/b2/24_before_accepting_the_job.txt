Before Accepting the Job
A: The hotel has offered me the job, but they want an answer tomorrow.
B: Congratulations! Are you happy with the offer?
A: Mostly. The pay's better, and I'd be in charge of the breakfast crew. But the letter doesn't say how many hours I'd get each week.
B: Is that because the job's newly created? Did they mention the hours at the interview?
A: They said around thirty. My absolute minimum is twenty-five. Do you think I can get that in writing?
B: It's worth asking. Have they sent you a contract yet?
A: No, just the offer letter, without the full contract terms.
B: And if they can't promise twenty-five hours, would you turn it down?
A: I'd hate to. They offer leadership training and there's a chance of promotion to a more senior position.
B: Would you still want this job if that took a few years?
A: Yes, if the hours were reliable. I liked the people I met on Saturday, and I'd enjoy working with them.
B: Who sent the offer? Could you ask them how the hotel works out the hours?
A: It came from a consultant at the agency. She said I could call her with questions.
B: Call her today. She may need to speak to the hotel before she can give you an answer.
A: I'll do that. It was the constant change of hours that made my last workplace so difficult. I'd like to settle this now.
B: Good idea. Then you'll have something clear to base your decision on.

Vocabulary: absolute, consultant, crew, leadership, minimum, newly, promotion, senior, terms, workplace
