News from the Market
A: Could you look at the page about the new market before I send it to the printer? This is our first publication, and I don't want to miss anything.
B: Sure. Is the editorial group happy with it?
A: Mostly. They've asked for your input because you know the people at the other stalls.
B: The opening says your cousin's cakes are the best in town. Have you tried all the others?
A: No. They're my favourite, but I see your point. That sounds like bias.
B: We need a more balanced introduction. Everyone with a stall paid the same amount towards the printing.
A: You're right. They should get the same publicity. I'll give each stall a short description and remove that claim.
B: Good. Where did the number of stalls come from? I thought there were twenty, not eighteen.
A: The market manager sent an updated list yesterday. Two people pulled out.
B: Then cite that list, with its date, so people know where the information came from.
A: Will do. What about the graphic showing where the stalls are? Can you read the names?
B: Most of them, but the names along the bottom are very small.
A: I could make the map larger and put the opening times beside it.
B: That would help. Could you incorporate a note about the entrance from the car park too?
A: Yes. People may not realize they can enter from that side. What have we arranged for distribution?
B: The library and the shops around the square have agreed to take copies. I'll deliver them on Thursday.
A: Thanks. I'll send you the revised page tonight so you can check the stall names before it goes to print.
B: Fine. I'll compare them with the manager's list too.

Vocabulary: balanced, bias, cite, distribution, editorial, graphic, incorporate, input, publication, publicity, stall
