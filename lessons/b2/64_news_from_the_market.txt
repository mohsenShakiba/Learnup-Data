News from the Market
A: Have you had a chance to read my article on the new street market? It goes to print tomorrow.
B: Yes, I've just finished it. Overall it reads well, but the editorial team thinks the first half shows a bit of bias.
A: Really? In what way?
B: You only interviewed people who run a stall. Naturally, they're happy about the extra publicity and sales.
A: That's true. I spent most of Saturday talking to them.
B: To make it more balanced, we also need some input from the shops around the square. Several managers were worried about losing parking spaces.
A: I actually spoke to two of those shop managers yesterday. I could incorporate a few lines from them about how the market has affected their trade.
B: Good. You also wrote that visitor numbers in the square have increased by five hundred on Saturdays. Where did that figure come from?
A: From the local government's monthly report. They counted visitors at the car park and the station.
B: Then cite that report, with its date, so people know the figure comes from official data.
A: Will do. What about the graphic at the bottom of the page? Is the map of the square clear enough?
B: Mostly, but the street names along the bottom are very small.
A: I can make the map slightly larger and put the market opening times beside it.
B: That should fix it. Once this page is ready, our first publication will be complete.
A: Have we arranged everything for distribution on Thursday?
B: Yes. The library, the station and the cafes around the square have all agreed to take copies.
A: Great. I'll add the shop managers' comments and the report details tonight, then send you the revised page.
B: Thanks. I'll check it first thing in the morning before I send the file to print.

Vocabulary: balanced, bias, cite, distribution, editorial, graphic, incorporate, input, publication, publicity, stall
