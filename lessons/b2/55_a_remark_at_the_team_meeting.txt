When a Colleague Copies My Accent
A: Do you have a minute before you leave the office? I'd like to talk about today's meeting.
B: Yes. Let's go into this empty room.
A: Mark copied my accent again when I was answering a question. I asked him to stop doing that last week.
B: I heard him. What did he say last week when you asked him to stop?
A: He said he didn't mean to offend me. Then he did it again today and made my answer sound ridiculous.
B: Has he done that to anyone else?
A: Yes, to two other people from my country. If we ask him to stop, he says we have no sense of humour. I think it's racism.
B: If you're worried it's racist, you can say that. You've already asked him to stop. How did it affect the rest of the meeting?
A: I felt deeply embarrassed today. I stopped explaining my idea because everyone was laughing.
B: I'm sorry I didn't say anything. Would you like me to talk to him?
A: Thanks, but I think I'll speak to our manager. I need to be able to speak openly in meetings without worrying about being laughed at.
B: Would you like me to come with you? I can tell her what I heard.
A: Yes, please. Last time I raised a problem she said I was looking for trouble. I've lost a bit of faith in her. I don't think my description would do justice to the way he spoke.
B: Then I'll describe what happened too. We can ask her to look into this particular incident and tell us what she'll do next.
A: Yes, let's ask her that. I also want to finish explaining my idea at the next meeting.
B: You can ask her for time to do that when we see her.
A: Good idea. I'll ask when she's free to talk to us.
B: Let me know what time she gives you.

Vocabulary: accent, deeply, faith, incident, justice, offend, openly, racism, racist, ridiculous
