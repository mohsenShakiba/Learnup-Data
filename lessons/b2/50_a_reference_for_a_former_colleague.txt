A Reference for a Former Colleague
A: Could I put your name down as a referee for a job? Each applicant has to give two names.
B: Of course. What's the job?
A: It's in advertising, at a small company. It has a reputation for giving people responsibility quite early.
B: That could suit you. You're skilled at working out what a customer needs without much help.
A: That's one of the things they asked about. The main criterion is whether I can use my own judgement on a project.
B: I can give examples from our work together. You met your deadlines consistently too.
A: Thanks. They may also ask about my technical expertise. I've been using some different software since I left.
B: I'll need to acknowledge that I haven't seen that work. How long has it been since you left us?
A: Nearly two years. My current manager is the other referee, so she can talk about the newer projects.
B: Good. Does the form ask about a weakness as well?
A: Yes. I was hoping you wouldn't mention how nervous I used to get during presentations.
B: But you improved. I could explain how you practised and then gave the final presentation to that big customer.
A: I'd be happy with that. I don't want you to pretend everything was perfect.
B: I won't. I genuinely think you did good work here, and I have examples to support that.
A: I'll send you the job description and the date they need the reference. Is your phone number still the same?
B: Yes. Send them in a message, and let me know when you've submitted the application.

Vocabulary: acknowledge, applicant, consistently, criterion, expertise, genuinely, judgement, reputation, skilled, weakness
