Choosing Photos for the Art Show
A: We have space for six large photos on the library wall. Which ones should we choose from your collection?
B: Definitely this winter landscape of the hills; the morning light on the snow is gorgeous.
A: I agree. We should put something very colourful beside it, like your picture of the fishing boats in the harbour.
B: Doesn't that look too ordinary next to the others? I wanted the display to feel more artistic.
A: The harbour picture has a very distinct look because of the way the bright paint reflects in the dark water.
B: True. What do you think of this abstract photo of the glass roof at the station?
A: I love it. The crossed lines create an extraordinary visual effect, almost like a fine net.
B: Yes, it gives the illusion that the roof is floating in the air when you stand back from it.
A: That careful use of light and shadow is a real characteristic of your best work.
B: Thanks. For the last space, shall we include the picture of the old tree in the square?
A: Yes, the low branches give it a unique shape, so visitors won't confuse it with anyone else's photos.
B: Good. Let's put the six frames in order on the table before we hang them up.

Vocabulary: abstract, artistic, characteristic, colourful, distinct, extraordinary, gorgeous, illusion, landscape, unique, visual
