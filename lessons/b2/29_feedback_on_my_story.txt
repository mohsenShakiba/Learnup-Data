Feedback on My Story
A: Have you had a chance to read my story? I want to submit it for the college website tomorrow.
B: Yes. I liked the humour in the first scene, but I wasn't sure how to interpret the ending.
A: Was it the missing letter? I wanted that to seem mysterious.
B: The letter was fine. I couldn't understand why the brother suddenly admitted taking it.
A: He feels guilty. Did that not come across?
B: Not quite. His tone is cheerful even when she finds the letter. Is she wrong about how he feels?
A: Yes. We only see him through his sister's viewpoint. I was trying to convey the idea that he's hiding his feelings.
B: Could you show him trying to tell her earlier? Then his decision at the end would make more sense.
A: Would that make the ending too predictable?
B: I don't think so. You wouldn't have to reveal what he'd done. He could just ask to speak to her, and she could refuse.
A: That would fit their argument in the kitchen. I kept that scene short, but perhaps it needs more space.
B: Was that a deliberate choice?
A: Yes. I was worried the story was getting too long.
B: You could cut some of the description of the house instead. I wanted to hear more of their conversation.
A: That's useful criticism. I'll try changing those two scenes tonight.
B: Send me the new version if you'd like. I'd be happy to read the ending again.
