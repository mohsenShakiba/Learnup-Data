The Autumn Choir Concert
A: I'm glad we finally have a fifteen-minute interval. We've been singing since seven o'clock, and my throat feels dry.
B: Mine too, but the choir sounds much stronger tonight. Everyone has been showing real enthusiasm during the fast songs.
A: How did the new autumn piece sound from your row?
B: The tune is lovely, though we still lose our concentration near the end when the rhythm changes.
A: That part is difficult because the orchestra plays so loudly behind us. If they kept their volume down a little, we'd hear our own notes better.
B: Wait until Helen speaks to them after the break. Since she started to conduct both groups together, the balance has improved a lot.
A: True. And she asked us to pause for two seconds before the final part so the words are clear.
B: Have you learned every lyric of the Italian song without looking at the page?
A: Nearly. I've been listening to an audio recording in the car all week to get the warm tone right.
B: Who wrote that piece originally?
A: A young composer from Milan, who used to compose music for theatre shows.
B: It's a very passionate song. Knowing that the local station will broadcast Friday's concert live makes me a bit nervous, though!
A: Don't worry, we've practised enough. Let's get some water before the second half begins.

Vocabulary: audio, broadcast, choir, compose, composer, concentration, conduct, enthusiasm, interval, lyric, orchestra, passionate, pause, rhythm, tone, tune, volume
