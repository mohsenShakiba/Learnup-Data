New Rules for Work Computers
A: Have you managed to change your computer password yet? Mine keeps saying my new choice isn't strong enough.
B: It took me three tries. You need at least twelve characters, with a combination of capital letters, numbers, and marks.
A: That's much more complicated than the old eight-letter ones. How am I supposed to remember it?
B: Write a line from a song you know and change a few letters into numbers. Why did the IT team impose all these new rules today?
A: Someone at headquarters opened a fake message yesterday. It nearly exposed the company's customer records to thieves.
B: No wonder they're worried about security threats. Did you see the second part of the instructions?
A: About using our phones? I haven't read that far yet.
B: Whenever you work away from the office, you have to enable a check that sends a six-number message to your phone instantly.
A: So nobody can get into my account with just the password?
B: Right. And they've changed the rules on saving files, too. The new policy prohibits copying company records onto a small disk or personal drive.
A: That makes sense. Someone could easily leave one of those on a train.
B: Exactly. Every manager has been asked to reinforce that rule at this week's team meetings.
A: Where should I save the files for tomorrow's presentation, then?
B: Put them in the shared space on the internal network. Try your new password first, and I'll show you where it is.

Vocabulary: combination, complicated, disk, enable, expose, headquarters, impose, instantly, password, prohibit, reinforce, threat
