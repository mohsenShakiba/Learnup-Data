The Door That Won't Lock
A: Thanks for coming. The washing machine's here. It stopped in the middle of every wash last week. Now it won't even start.
B: You mentioned a message on the display when you called. What does it say?
A: It says the door's open, but it seems properly shut. Could something be wrong with the electronics?
B: Possibly. There's a rubber ring round the door. If the ring slips, the machine can't operate properly. Can you show me what happens when you switch it on?
A: Here, look. I push the door shut, I hear a click, and the same message comes up.
B: I see it. Which mode did you choose? There's a setting that holds the water in the machine until you tell it to empty.
A: The ordinary wash. Should I try the command on the screen that says "Empty"?
B: Give me a moment to check the door. That's the critical thing if there's water inside.
A: Can you still get parts for this model?
B: I'll check. This model was sold widely, so there's a good chance we still have that lock in stock.
A: One other thing: the clothes have been coming out wetter than usual.
B: For how long?
A: A few weeks. There's a bad smell too. I've seen cleaners for washing machines. This one says it uses a mild acid. Would that help?
B: Let's check whether the machine is clearing the water efficiently first. A cleaner might help with the smell from bacteria, but it won't fix a broken part.
A: Please do. It's six years old. Would I be better off buying a new one?
B: Let's get an estimate first. A new machine could cost ten times what one small part costs, and I'd rather tell you for sure before you spend that.

Vocabulary: acid, bacteria, command, critical, efficiently, electronics, mode, operate, rubber, widely
