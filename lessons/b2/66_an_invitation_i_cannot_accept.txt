An Invitation I Cannot Accept
A: Have you replied to Nina's wedding invitation? She asked me about it yesterday.
B: Not yet. I really want to go, but I can't afford the trip and two nights at a hotel.
A: Could you stay with someone nearby?
B: No, I don't know anyone nearby. And the hotel cost is only partly the problem—I'd also lose two days' pay.
A: That's a lot. You don't have to spend money you don't have. Are you worried she'll take it personally?
B: A bit. When I came out as gay, she was the first person in the family I told. I know what a disappointment it was for her when we missed each other's birthdays last year too.
A: She knows how close you are. Tell her you'd love to be there and explain why you can't.
B: Wouldn't it be easier to make an excuse about work? I don't want to discuss my money problems.
A: You can keep the details private. Don't let it become a big family affair if you'd prefer just to talk to Nina.
B: She might insist on paying for my ticket. She offered when I visited last year.
A: Would you be comfortable accepting that?
B: No. She's already paying for a wedding. I don't want to make it her problem.
A: Then tell her that. She'll know you're truly sorry. And it's easier to have that conversation if you speak to her before they have to confirm the numbers.
B: You're right. She needs to confirm the numbers. I'll call her tonight.
A: Good. Is she coming back here before the wedding?
B: Yes, next month. I could cook dinner for her and Ben here. Our parents' divorce was hard on both of us, and I miss spending time with her on my own terms.
A: That sounds lovely. She's always telling people your chocolate cake tastes like heaven. That recipe's your one claim to fame in the family!
B: She does! I'll invite them for dinner and a proper catch-up when I call tonight.

Vocabulary: affair, disappointment, divorce, excuse, fame, gay, heaven, insist, partly, truly
