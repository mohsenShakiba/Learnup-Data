An Invitation I Cannot Accept
A: Have you replied to Nina's wedding invitation? She asked me about it yesterday.
B: Not yet. I desperately want to go, but I can't afford the journey and two nights in a hotel.
A: Could you stay with someone nearby?
B: I've asked. Everyone I know has a full house that weekend. The cost is partly the problem, but I'd also lose two days' pay.
A: That's a lot. You aren't under an obligation to spend money you don't have.
B: I know. I just hate the thought of her disappointment if I decline.
A: She's your friend. Tell her you'd love to be there and explain why you can't.
B: Wouldn't it be easier to make an excuse about work? I don't want to discuss my money problems.
A: You can be sincere without giving her every detail. Just say the trip is more than you can manage right now.
B: She might insist on paying for my ticket. She offered when I visited last year.
A: Would you be comfortable accepting that?
B: No. She's already paying for a wedding. I don't want to make it her problem.
A: Then say that too, kindly. It's unfortunate that you can't go, but keeping her waiting won't make it easier.
B: You're right. She needs to confirm the numbers. I'll call her tonight.
A: Good. Is she coming back here before the wedding?
B: Yes, next month. I could cook dinner for her and Ben here. It would be a small gesture, but we'd have time to talk.
A: That sounds nice. A quiet dinner might be just what they need before the wedding.
B: I'll ask her tonight, once I've explained why I can't come.

Vocabulary: decline, desperately, disappointment, excuse, gesture, insist, obligation, partly, sincere, unfortunate
