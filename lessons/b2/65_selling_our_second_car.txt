Selling Our Second Car
A: The insurance and road tax for my car are both due next month. Since we pay them annually, that's nearly eight hundred pounds at once.
B: Already? It feels like we only just paid them. And you barely drive that car nowadays.
A: Exactly. Since I started working from home four days a week, it basically sits outside the house from Monday to Friday.
B: Are you thinking we should get rid of it and just share mine?
A: I am. Having two cars was a necessity when we worked in different towns, but keeping both now costs a fortune.
B: Has your manager confirmed that you can work from home permanently, though?
A: Yes, she signed the new contract today. I only have to go into the office on Wednesdays.
B: How would you get there on Wednesdays? I still need my car for work every day.
A: You pass the station on your way. Could you drop me there at eight and pick me up on your way home?
B: That would work. What if one of us needs to go somewhere in the evening or at the weekend?
A: We'll just need to check our plans with each other first. On the rare days we both need to travel, one of us can take a taxi.
B: True. Even a few taxis a month would be much cheaper than paying for two cars. Selling yours would really lower our regular bills.
A: And we could put the money from the sale aside for our summer holiday, or for repairs on your car.
B: Do you think we'll get a decent price for it? It's eight years old now.
A: The mechanic said small automatic cars are in high demand when he checked it last month. Plus, it's still in reasonably good condition.
B: Let's clean it inside and out this Saturday, then. Afterwards, we can take some photos and advertise it online.
A: Good idea. If we sell it before the end of the month, we won't have to pay for another year's tax and insurance.

Vocabulary: annually, aside, basically, demand, fortune, lower, necessity, nowadays, permanently, reasonably, rid
