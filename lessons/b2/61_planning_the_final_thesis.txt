Planning the Final Thesis
A: Thanks for looking at my history thesis outline. Do you think the topic is too wide?
B: A little. At the moment, it comprises three different decades. You'll get a stronger argument if you concentrate on one decade and examine every aspect of it properly.
A: That makes sense. I was worried I wouldn't have enough information if I chose a shorter period.
B: There are numerous local records from that decade in the university library. Have you consulted the collection list yet?
A: Only the online list. I saw several letters from factory managers that precede the new working laws.
B: Good. Those letters can help you test your main hypothesis about why working hours changed when they did.
A: Should I still include the work of modern scholars in the first section?
B: Yes. Cite the historians who have written on the subject, and evaluate how clear their evidence is.
A: One of them only studied large cities. Can I point out that limitation in my evaluation of his book?
B: Definitely. Explaining what earlier studies missed will show why your own research is useful.
A: How many sources should the first chapter include?
B: Around ten is enough at this stage, as long as each one is relevant to your question.
A: Right. I'll narrow the outline to that decade and check which boxes of records I need to order.
B: Bring the revised plan to next Tuesday's meeting, and we'll look at your chapter titles together.

Vocabulary: aspect, cite, comprise, consult, evaluate, evaluation, hypothesis, limitation, numerous, precede, scholar, thesis
