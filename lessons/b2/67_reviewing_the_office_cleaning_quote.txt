Choosing a Film for Friday Night
A: Are we still taking Grandad to the cinema on Friday? He saw a poster for that new film about the fighting in his village and asked me to book.
B: I haven't booked yet. Your cousin's staying with us on Friday, remember? Is it something a twelve-year-old could watch?
A: I'm not sure. Grandad thought it was about life in a village before the invasion. Let me check the cinema website.
B: What's the age limit? That's the first thing I'd look for.
A: Fifteen. It says there are frequent scenes of violence. We can't take her to that one.
B: No. And does Grandad know about those scenes? He walked out in the middle of that military film last year.
A: The bombing upset him. He said hearing the planes brought back the terror he felt as a boy. He was quiet the whole way home.
B: There's another film about the same village. I heard the woman who made it on television talking about the destruction of her family's home.
A: That sounds pretty depressing for a Friday evening too. Is there anything my cousin actually wants to see?
B: She wants to see the one about two sisters lost in the mountains. It's a survival story, but she says it's more funny than scary.
A: The one about the older sister who's blind? Grandad read the book. He thought it was really moving. It has a happy ending, too. He normally goes for tragic stories.
B: That's the one. There's a funny bit in the ad where she can hear the people coming to help long before her sister can see them.
A: When does it start? Grandad doesn't want to drive home in the dark.
B: Half past two, and it ends at four. I could pick them both up. Shall I call him and see what he thinks?
A: Yes. Tell him we can go to the other film together next week if he still wants to. He might choose something else once he's heard about the sound.
B: Good idea. I'll check with them both before I spend anything.
A: And see if he'd like to have an early dinner here after the film. He hasn't seen her for ages.
B: Will do. He's probably going to ask if he can bring his book for her to borrow.

Vocabulary: bombing, depressing, destruction, invasion, military, moving, survival, terror, tragic, violence
