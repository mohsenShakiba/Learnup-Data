Talking to My Daughter's Teacher
A: Thanks for making time to see me after school. I wanted to ask how Ella's getting on in English this term.
B: Of course. Come in and take a seat. I'm glad you came in, actually.
A: Her marks have dropped this term, and she's worried about her university place. Is she struggling with the work?
B: Her creative writing is excellent, but she hasn't handed in her last three assignments. If that continues, she could fail the course.
A: Three? I see her working most evenings. Has she told you what's going on?
B: She says she keeps starting again because nothing seems good enough. I've asked her to show me her work before it's finished.
A: She does that at home too. She's constantly changing the same page.
B: She has a wonderful imagination. I want to encourage that creativity, though I can't give her marks for work I haven't seen.
A: Is she afraid you'll think her work is poor?
B: She can be very hard on herself. If I say one thing isn't clear, she thinks I'm criticizing the whole piece.
A: I hadn't realised it had got that bad. Can she still catch up?
B: Yes. Those assignments help my pupils prepare for the summer examinations. Ella read us the opening of a story about a temple in class. I'd like to see the rest of it.
A: Should she try to finish all three this weekend?
B: Let's just finish one. In that scene, everyone walks around the same point in the temple. She writes that it's holy, but she never says why. I want the story behind it.
A: She's told me that part! There's a legend about a child who heard someone call his name in an empty room. Nobody believed him until his little sister heard it too.
B: That's a unique detail. Ask her to tell it to me the way she told you. The story's there already.
A: Could she help decide the new deadlines?
B: Yes. We'll look at the work together and agree on dates she can manage.

Vocabulary: constantly, creativity, criticize, examination, holy, imagination, legend, pupil, temple, unique
