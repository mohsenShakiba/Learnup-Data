Talking to My Daughter's Teacher
A: Thanks for meeting me. Ella was upset about her reading mark. Is she falling behind the other pupils?
B: No. Her reading is strong, and her imagination is outstanding. She sometimes adds things that aren't in the text, though.
A: She enjoys inventing stories. Is creativity less important in this year's curriculum?
B: We still encourage it. But this task asked her to explain a character's decision, rather than write a new story.
A: She said her interpretation was different from yours. Did that make her answer wrong?
B: A different interpretation can be fine. Her explanation needs to be logical and supported by something in the passage.
A: Could you show me where it went wrong?
B: Here. She says the boy left because he was angry with his sister, but the story never mentions a sister.
A: Ah, I see. She filled in the missing information herself.
B: Exactly. I praised her for noticing that he seemed angry. Now she needs to explain what in the story suggests that.
A: Will she have to do this in the examination too?
B: Yes. The emphasis is on explaining an answer, not just choosing one.
A: How can I help without telling her what to write?
B: When you read together, ask which sentence helped her decide how a character feels. Let her show you.
A: We could try that with the book she's reading tonight.
B: Good. I'll go through this passage with her tomorrow so she can try her answer again.

Vocabulary: creativity, curriculum, emphasis, examination, imagination, interpretation, logical, outstanding, praise, pupil
