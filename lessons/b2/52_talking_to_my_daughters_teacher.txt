Talking to My Daughter's Teacher
A: Thanks for seeing me after school. I'd like to talk about my daughter Ella's English work now she's in her final year of secondary school.
B: Of course. Come in and take a seat. Let's talk about how she's getting on.
A: Her marks have dropped this term, and she's worried about her university place. Is she struggling with the work?
B: Her creative writing is outstanding, but she hasn't handed in her last three assignments. If that continues, she could fail the course.
A: Three? I see her working most evenings. Has she told you what's going on?
B: She says she keeps starting again because nothing seems good enough. I've asked to see her drafts, but she hasn't brought them in.
A: She has a tendency to do that at home too. She's constantly changing the same page.
B: She has a wonderful imagination. I want to encourage that creativity, but I can't give her marks for work I haven't seen.
A: Is she afraid you'll think her work is poor?
B: She told me she was. I've praised her writing before, but she still worries about disappointing me.
A: I hadn't realised it had got that bad. Can she still catch up?
B: Yes. The assignments are part of the curriculum and help pupils prepare for the final examination. She needs to finish them, but we can agree on a plan.
A: Should she try to finish all three this weekend?
B: No. The emphasis should be on finishing one piece first. I'd like her to bring what she's written so far tomorrow, even if she isn't happy with it.
A: I'll talk to her tonight. I don't want to put more pressure on her.
B: Ask how she's feeling about it, and let her know she doesn't need to finish everything before our meeting.
A: Could she help decide the new deadlines?
B: Yes. We'll look at the work together and agree on dates she can manage.

Vocabulary: constantly, creativity, curriculum, emphasis, examination, imagination, outstanding, praise, pupil, tendency
