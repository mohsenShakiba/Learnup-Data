Editing the College Paper
A: The printer needs the final pages of the college paper by five o'clock. How much space do we have left?
B: None at all. In fact, the total volume of text is still two pages too long for this issue.
A: Which section can we cut down without spoiling the publication?
B: My editorial on the first page is longer than usual. I can cut the last part and still keep the main point.
A: Thanks. In that piece, I like how you emphasize the work of the student volunteers rather than just the exam results.
B: I didn't want the opening page to criticize the college rules again; we did enough of that last term.
A: What about the third page? Can we incorporate the new library hours at the bottom of the page?
B: Yes, if we make the graphic beside the sports report a little smaller.
A: Should we use bold letters for the library times so people notice the change straight away?
B: Definitely. Have you checked the short comic drawing that Sam sent in for the back page?
A: Yes, it's really funny, and it fits the space above the page index nicely.
B: Our journalism teacher said this is the strongest issue we've produced all year.
A: Let's read through the page numbers one last time before we send the file to the printer.

Vocabulary: bold, comic, criticize, editorial, emphasize, graphic, incorporate, index, journalism, publication, volume
