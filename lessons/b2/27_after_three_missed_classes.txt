After Three Missed Classes
A: I've missed three classes, and I'm not sure where to start. Is Friday's report still due this week?
B: For most students, yes, but you can have an extension until Monday because you've been ill.
A: Thank you. I've been trying to catch up with the textbook reading, but I'm still getting tired quite quickly.
B: Just read the two chapters on the bus experiment for now. Start with the one about the concept behind the experiment. The next one describes what actually happened.
A: My copy is an older edition. Are the chapter numbers the same?
B: No. Look for the chapters called "City Life" and "Public Transport". I'll write down the titles for you.
A: That helps. Do I need to include everything from both chapters?
B: Stick to one question. Did more people take the bus when the tickets were cheaper? Give me your own reading of the findings.
A: Could I use the chart showing the decrease in car journeys? I take the bus myself now it's cheaper.
B: Yes. Give a detailed example from your journey. Then look at when people made the change. Was the drop rapid or did it happen slowly?
A: Is there a particular format for the report?
B: Follow the plan on this sheet. You'll find an example below it.
A: Thanks. Do I have to give my own evaluation of the experiment at the end? I take the bus, but most of my friends still drive.
B: Yes. Tell me who it worked for and why. You don't have to say it worked for everybody.
A: All right. Do I send the report through the course website?
B: Yes. I'll change your deadline there today so it shows Monday.

Vocabulary: concept, deadline, decrease, edition, evaluation, extension, finding, format, rapid, textbook
