A Private Vote for the Building Meeting
A: Are we choosing someone to run the building committee at Thursday's annual meeting? I saw three names on the notice.
B: Yes. Martin's had enough of doing it, and three neighbours have offered to take over. I'm sending out their names today.
A: Will we choose by raising our hands? There's been some disagreement about that.
B: I was going to suggest it. Would people prefer to write their choice on paper?
A: I think so. Half the point of voting on paper is that you can choose someone without upsetting the neighbour who lives next door.
B: Then let's use paper so people can vote freely. We shouldn't ask people to write their name, age or gender on it. All we need is their choice.
A: Who will count the papers?
B: Jo and I can do it together. Neither of us is standing.
A: What happens if two people get the same number of votes?
B: We'll have another vote between those two. I'd like an observer from each building to watch the count. Then people can feel confident it's fair.
A: Good. Am I allowed to put the names in order? I'd love to rank all three. My second choice would be fine too.
B: No, just one name. Otherwise it gets complicated. I'll write that at the top of the paper.
A: What if someone needs help reading the names? Mum asked me to vote for her last year. I wasn't sure I'd handled it appropriately.
B: They can bring someone they trust. A neighbour helped my mum with hers. She could see the names but couldn't hold the pen.
A: Good. There was almost universal support for bigger print when we asked about it last year. Can everyone have the larger version this time?
B: Yes, everyone can have that version. I'll put a few chairs by the table too, so people can sit down while they read.
A: Please. I'd like us to unite the two buildings over something for a change. People keep saying it's us against them. Can we do anything to encourage a sense of community?
B: Then let's start by asking everyone together about the repairs on Thursday. I'll make time for that on the agenda.

Vocabulary: annual, appropriately, disagreement, freely, gender, observer, rank, unite, universal, voting
