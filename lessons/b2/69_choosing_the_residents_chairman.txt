Choosing the Residents' Chairman
A: Are we still going to elect a new chairman at Thursday's residents' meeting?
B: Yes. Martin's decided to resign, and three people have offered to take over. We're sending their names and short statements to everyone today.
A: Will we choose by raising our hands? There's been some disagreement about that.
B: I was going to suggest it. Would people prefer to write their choice on paper?
A: I think so. Some residents are worried about upsetting a neighbour if they choose someone else.
B: Then let's use paper, so people can choose freely.
A: Who will count the papers?
B: Jo and I can do it together. Neither of us is standing.
A: What happens if two people get the same number of votes?
B: We can have another vote between those two. That needs to be explained before anyone votes, though.
A: Agreed. When will you declare the result?
B: At the end of the meeting, once we've both checked the totals.
A: Good. The new chairman needs to listen to residents in both blocks. I'm worried the bigger block will dominate the meeting again.
B: We're a minority in the smaller block, but we still need a say. What would you like the new chairman to do?
A: Ask both blocks about repairs before deciding which ones come first. Martin's only asked his own block.
B: You could ask all three candidates about it on Thursday, before the vote.
A: I will. I'd be more optimistic about the outcome if I knew they were taking our repairs seriously.
B: I'll add time for residents' questions to the agenda, then. Come a little early so we can check how many people want to speak.

Vocabulary: chairman, declare, disagreement, dominate, elect, freely, minority, optimistic, outcome, resign
