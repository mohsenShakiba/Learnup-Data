Training a New Colleague
A: How's the new junior assistant getting on? You've been training him this week, haven't you?
B: Yes. He's capable, but I think I've been giving him too much information at once.
A: What happened?
B: I explained the program we use for orders, then asked him to enter five orders. He made the same mistake on all five.
A: Had he watched you enter a complete order first?
B: No. He'd used a similar program before, so my assumption was that he knew the basic steps.
A: They're probably different here. Perhaps he could observe you this morning and then try one himself.
B: That would help. Initially, I thought he was just being slow, and I got a bit impatient.
A: Did he ask any questions?
B: A few, but I was answering calls too. I told him not to interrupt while I was on the phone.
A: Then he may hesitate to ask again. Could you spend half an hour with him away from the phones?
B: Yes, after ten. I should explain my expectations more clearly too. He doesn't need to match my speed yet.
A: Exactly. A thorough check of each order matters more at this stage. He'll become more efficient with practice.
B: I'll tell him that. Could you take my calls from ten to half past?
A: Of course. I'll let the others know you're training him.
B: Thanks. I'll start by asking him which part of the program he's not sure about.

Vocabulary: assumption, capable, efficient, expectation, hesitate, impatient, initially, interrupt, junior, observe, thorough
