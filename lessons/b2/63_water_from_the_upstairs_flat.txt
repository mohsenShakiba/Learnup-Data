Water from the Upstairs Flat
A: Sorry to disturb you, Ben, but water is coming through our bathroom ceiling. Could you check around your bath?
B: Oh no, come in. My son has just had a bath, and he did spill some water on the floor.
A: Would a little water on the floor go all the way through the ceiling, though?
B: Wait—let me feel the pipe behind the bath panel. It's wet, and water is falling downwards from the joint.
A: So the pipe itself is the problem? Our ceiling was newly painted last month, and a dark mark is already spreading.
B: I'm really sorry. Let me grab some old towels and wrap them around the pipe while I turn off the water tap under the floor.
A: Thanks. Has it stopped now that you've turned the tap?
B: Not completely, because there's still water in the pipe, so drops are falling constantly onto the towel.
A: At least the cause is apparent now. We'll need a repair worker before you can turn the water back on.
B: I'll call one immediately. And I can assure you that I'll pay for any work your ceiling needs.
A: Thank you. It's hard to judge the full extent of the damage until the ceiling dries out.
B: The flow has slowed down somewhat now. Can I come downstairs in ten minutes and look at your ceiling?
A: Yes, please. The mark hasn't grown since you turned the tap off, hence I think we caught it in time.
B: Good. I'll put a bowl under this joint now and ring the emergency repair company before I come down.

Vocabulary: apparent, assure, constantly, downwards, extent, grab, hence, newly, somewhat, spill, wrap
