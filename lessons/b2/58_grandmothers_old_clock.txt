Grandmother's Old Clock
A: Have you decided what to do with the clock we inherited from Grandma? It's still in the box by the door.
B: I'd like to keep it, but we don't have much space. Did you want it?
A: Yes. It's precious to me. She used to let me turn the little key when I visited.
B: I remember. She kept it for most of her lifetime. Do you know anything about its origin?
A: Only that it came from her parents' house. There's a photo of our ancestors with that clock on the shelf behind them.
B: Then we should keep that photo with it. Otherwise, nobody will know the connection later.
A: Agreed. I don't know whether the clock has much financial value.
B: Does that matter to you?
A: Not much. My reason for keeping it is purely emotional. I'd treasure it even if it never worked again.
B: It is charming. It would be a pity to leave it in a box. I'd like to hear it working, just as it did in her kitchen.
A: I could have it checked before I put it on my shelf. I don't want to damage it by trying to repair it myself.
B: There's a clock shop near the station. We could take it there and ask about the cost.
A: Would you be happy for me to keep it at my place, then?
B: Yes. I have her paintings, and I can see the clock when I visit you.
A: Come with me to the shop on Saturday. We can take the photo too.
B: I'd like that. I'll ask Dad whether he remembers when her family bought it.

Vocabulary: ancestor, charming, emotional, inherit, lifetime, origin, pity, precious, purely, treasure
