Exercises for a Stiff Shoulder
A: Hello. Come in and take a seat. Is this your first session of therapy for your shoulder?
B: Yes. My doctor sent me because I've had a lot of pain in my right shoulder and neck for the last three weeks.
A: Raise your arm slowly for me. Does that motion hurt?
B: It pulls at the top of my shoulder, and my neck feels very tight by the end of the day.
A: There's quite a bit of tension in these muscles, but the shoulder joint itself is moving normally. Do you work at a computer?
B: Yes, all day. I noticed yesterday that I lean forward when I'm reading small text on the monitor.
A: That puts extra weight on your neck and shoulders. Could you move your monitor closer and raise your chair slightly?
B: I can do that at home, yes. What can I do to relieve the pain when it starts?
A: A warm towel helps at first, and I'll demonstrate a short sequence of exercises for you to do twice a day.
B: Will they take long? It's hard to stop for half an hour during the working day.
A: Five minutes is enough. Doing a little every day is more beneficial than one long session at the weekend.
B: Watch me try the first one. Should I pull my shoulders back like this?
A: Keep the movement gentle and breathe steadily while you hold it for five seconds.
B: That feels better already. How soon will these exercises strengthen the muscles?
A: You should notice a difference in two weeks. Book another visit at the desk, and if I'm away that day, the other therapist here can check your shoulder.

Vocabulary: beneficial, demonstrate, joint, lean, motion, relieve, sequence, steadily, strengthen, tension, therapist, therapy
