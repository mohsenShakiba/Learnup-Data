Shoulder Pain at Work
A: Hello. Come in and take a seat. Have you had any therapy for your shoulder before?
B: No. My doctor sent me because I've had a lot of pain in my right shoulder and neck for the last three weeks.
A: Raise your arm slowly for me. Does it hurt when you do that?
B: It pulls at the top of my shoulder, and my neck feels very tight by the end of the day.
A: Is the pain constant or does it come and go? You said on the form you work at a computer.
B: It's worst after lunch. I move right up to the monitor to read small text. I'm practically sitting on the edge of my seat by then.
A: Could you bring the screen closer or make the text bigger? Try that here with mine.
B: Oh, I can read from the back of the chair now. Is this where my elbow should be? My fingers sometimes hurt too.
A: Show me the motion you make when you reach for the mouse. Where do you feel the pull?
B: The pain's here in my shoulder. And when I get up after sitting like that, my hip hurts too. Could I be doing myself harm?
A: If a movement hurts, don't keep repeating it. You told me you had a fall last month. Was there any pain before that?
B: No. I landed hard on my hand, though. I had a red mark on my inner wrist for days.
A: I'd like the doctor to check that before we try exercises to strengthen your shoulder. Do you still have the note from your last visit?
B: Yes, on my phone. It says I should come back if I'm still suffering after two weeks. It's been three.
A: Then call today. Can I write down what you showed me so you can describe it accurately?
B: Please. Should I mention the pain was quite intense yesterday, even though it feels better now?
A: Yes. Tell them that too. Once the doctor's checked your wrist, we can arrange another visit with a therapist here.

Vocabulary: harm, hip, inner, intense, monitor, motion, strengthen, suffering, therapist, therapy
