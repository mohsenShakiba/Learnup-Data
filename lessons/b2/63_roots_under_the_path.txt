Roots Under the Path
A: Have you seen all the water by the side door? I tried to sweep it off the path this morning, but it keeps collecting against the house wall.
B: Yes, I noticed that after yesterday's rain. Two of the flat stones on the path have been pushed upwards.
A: What's caused that?
B: The roots from that large bush beside the fence. They've grown right under the path.
A: That explains it. Instead of flowing downwards towards the garden, the water is getting trapped between those two stones and the wall.
B: Exactly. And if water sits against the bricks all winter, it could harm the wall and make the living room cold and wet inside.
A: Doesn't the main water pipe run parallel to the path there too?
B: It does. If the roots reach that pipe, they could damage it. We really need to tackle the problem before the winter rain starts.
A: Could we put a chemical treatment on the roots, or do we have to dig the whole bush out?
B: I checked a root treatment at the garden shop, but the label says it's harmful to the soil and nearby plants. It could kill our grass and the neighbour's trees.
A: Then we'll have to cut the branches back and dig the bush out by hand.
B: Once we lift those two stones, we can cut through the roots under them and clear them out.
A: Will the stones sit flat again after that?
B: They should if we put a bag of sand under them and press it down well. That'll make the path level and stable again.
A: Let's buy a bag of sand tomorrow morning and start while the ground is still soft from the rain.
B: Good idea. I'll get the tools out of the shed after breakfast so we can lift the stones first.

Vocabulary: bush, dig, downwards, harm, harmful, parallel, root, stable, sweep, tackle, trap, upwards
