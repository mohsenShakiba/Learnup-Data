A Problem with a Shop Return
A: Did the kitchen shop agree to replace the soup maker that stopped working on Sunday?
B: No, and I'm still annoyed about how the manager spoke to me. It was a complete disappointment.
A: Why wouldn't he replace it? We only bought it three weeks ago, and we still have the receipt.
B: He claimed we must have dropped it, which is a dishonest excuse because there isn't a single mark on the outside.
A: Did he think you were a fool who wouldn't know your rights as a customer?
B: It felt like that. When I showed him the law on broken goods, he took offence and said he was too busy to argue.
A: That's no way to run a retail company. Did anyone else in the shop try to help?
B: The young assistant looked embarrassed and gave me a sincere apology at the door, but she wasn't allowed to return our money herself.
A: What a shame. I know how much you were looking forward to using it this week; I hate to see a shop disappoint a regular customer like that.
B: It's unfortunate, because I've bought several pans there before without any trouble.
A: I'd urge you to write to the company's head office tonight and attach a photo of the receipt.
B: Yes, if the branch manager won't handle a simple return appropriately, the head office needs to know.

Vocabulary: appropriately, disappoint, disappointment, dishonest, excuse, fool, offence, retail, shame, sincere, unfortunate, urge
