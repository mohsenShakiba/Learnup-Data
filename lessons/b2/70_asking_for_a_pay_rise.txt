Asking for a Pay Rise
A: Could we talk about my pay? I've been managing the new accounts in the games division for six months, and my salary hasn't changed.
B: Of course. Head office will need to know how your job has changed. Have you brought examples of your work on the new accounts?
A: Yes. I've listed all ten. The sales have gone up every month since I took them on.
B: I can see that. Orders from the two accounts you took over in April have grown dramatically, and you've gained a lot of trust with their teams.
A: Thanks. I'd like my pay to reflect that. I know the company isn't as wealthy as some of our competitors, but people doing the same job there earn quite a bit more.
B: I understand. I can't approve a rise myself—head office sets the salary bands—though these figures make a very convincing case.
A: Would they look at what I'm actually doing? My job description says I work with elementary schools on number games. Half my time now goes into handling our university accounts too.
B: Yes. I'll need to show what you're doing now compared with the job you were hired for.
A: That's what I'd hoped. The extra responsibility is a significant change.
B: Yes, it is. What about the extra payments we make on new accounts? Have they made much difference to your pay?
A: No. Most of my work is with existing customers. I also spend time helping the software team improve the interaction between the children and the games. That doesn't bring new customers in straight away.
B: Fair point. Is there a way to show how your work with the software team affects sales?
A: I can send you comments from customers who liked the changes and bought the next game. I enjoy that work. My motivation for bringing this up is that I'd like to keep doing it here.
B: Then let's make the case for a higher salary. Head office liked your work on the economics game last week. Can you include the sales for that one and the customer feedback?
A: Yes, including the comments from the pilot school. They were using it to teach the difference between a country's income and its wealth. When should I expect an answer?
B: I'll send it tomorrow. Let's meet next Friday, even if I'm still waiting for a decision.
A: Thanks. I'd appreciate an update either way.
B: Of course. I'll tell you what they've said and what happens next.

Vocabulary: division, dramatically, economics, elementary, gain, interaction, motivation, significant, wealth, wealthy
