Asking for a Pay Rise
A: Could we talk about my pay? I've been managing our larger corporate accounts for six months, and my salary hasn't changed.
B: Of course. Let's look at how your job has changed, so head office can evaluate your pay properly. Have you brought some examples of the extra work?
A: Yes, I've listed the accounts I've taken on and the sales for each month. The increase has been consistent, not just one unusually good month.
B: I can see that. Orders from the two accounts you took over in April have grown dramatically, and you've gained a lot of trust with their teams.
A: I'm really pleased about that—I'd just like my pay to reflect it, and show some recognition for the extra work. With inflation as high as it is, my usual bills are harder to manage.
B: I understand. I can't approve a rise myself—head office sets the salary bands—though these figures make a very convincing case.
A: Would they look at my current responsibilities as well as the sales figures?
B: Yes. I'll need to show what you're doing now compared with the job you were hired for.
A: That's what I'd hoped. The extra responsibility is a significant change.
B: Have you considered the sales incentive scheme? You could earn commission on new accounts.
A: I've read about it, though most of my work is with existing customers. I don't want to give them less attention just to earn more.
B: That's a fair concern. The scheme wouldn't reward much of your current work.
A: Exactly. Money isn't my only motivation, yet I need to know whether there's room for my salary to grow in this job.
B: Let me put forward the case for a higher salary first. Can you send me this list and a short description of the extra responsibilities?
A: Yes, this afternoon. When should I expect an answer?
B: I'll submit it tomorrow and ask when they can review it. Let's meet next Friday, even if I'm still waiting for a decision.
A: Thanks. I'd appreciate an update either way.
B: Of course. I'll tell you what they've said and what happens next.

Vocabulary: commission, consistent, corporate, dramatically, evaluate, gain, incentive, inflation, motivation, recognition, significant
