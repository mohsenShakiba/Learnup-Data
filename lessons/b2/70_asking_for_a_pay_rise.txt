Asking for a Pay Rise
A: Could we talk about my pay? I've been managing the larger customer accounts for six months, but my salary hasn't changed.
B: Yes. We should evaluate how your job has changed. Have you brought any examples of the extra work?
A: I've listed the accounts I've taken on and the sales for each month. The increase has been consistent, not just one unusually good month.
B: I can see that. The two accounts you took over in April are both ordering more now.
A: I'm pleased about that, but I'd like some recognition in my salary too. Inflation has made my usual bills harder to manage.
B: I understand. I can't approve a rise myself; the corporate office sets the salary ranges. I can seek approval, though.
A: Would they look at my current responsibilities as well as the sales figures?
B: Yes. I'll need to show what you're doing now compared with the job you were hired for.
A: That's what I'd hoped. The extra responsibility is a significant change, and I want the pay to reflect it.
B: Have you considered the sales incentive scheme? You could earn commission on new accounts.
A: I've read about it, but most of my work is with existing customers. I don't want to give them less attention just to earn more.
B: That's a fair concern. The scheme wouldn't reward much of your current work.
A: Exactly. Money isn't my only motivation, but I need to know whether there's room for my salary to grow in this job.
B: Let me make the case for a higher salary first. Can you send me this list and a short description of the extra responsibilities?
A: Yes, this afternoon. When should I expect an answer?
B: I'll submit it tomorrow and ask when they can review it. Let's meet next Friday, even if I'm still waiting for a decision.
A: Thanks. I'd appreciate an update either way.
B: Of course. I'll tell you what they've said and what happens next.

Vocabulary: commission, consistent, corporate, evaluate, incentive, inflation, motivation, recognition, seek, significant
