The Parking Rules
A: Did last night's meeting sort out the delivery trucks blocking our gate? I heard shouting from the hall.
B: Eventually, after quite a heated debate. Paul was still furious about waiting twenty minutes behind that furniture truck yesterday morning.
A: Oh, I missed that. Did the driver really leave it right across the entrance?
B: Yes. He claimed there was nowhere else to stop, but Paul accused him of deliberately ignoring the "No Parking" sign.
A: Well, whether he meant to or not, blocking the only way in and out is unacceptable. What if an ambulance needed to get through?
B: Exactly. Paul wanted to keep the main gate locked all day, but hardly anyone supported that idea.
A: No wonder. How would visitors or deliveries get in? What did people propose instead?
B: Turning the far end of the yard into a ten-minute delivery zone, well away from the gate.
A: Do we have enough space for that without losing residents' parking?
B: In the end, the majority voted to use the two visitor spaces beside the shops, and move visitor parking round the back.
A: That sounds sensible. My only concern is what happens if two trucks turn up at once.
B: With a ten-minute limit, the second driver shouldn't have to wait long, and the guard will make sure nobody stops across the gate.
A: Much better. When do the new rules start?
B: Next Monday. They're putting a notice up by the lifts today so every resident can see which spaces are changing.

Vocabulary: accuse, ambulance, concern, debate, deliberately, furious, majority, propose, resident, unacceptable, zone
