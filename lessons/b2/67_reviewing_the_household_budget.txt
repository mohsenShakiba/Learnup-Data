Reviewing the Household Budget
A: Have you looked at our account statement for this month? Our spending has exceeded my pay again.
B: We haven't bought anything unusual, though. Why is the total so much higher than it was last spring?
A: Mostly inflation. Our food and electricity bills have risen by a considerable amount over the year.
B: And the home insurance is paid annually in November, isn't it? That always takes a big part of this month's money.
A: Yes, that went out on Tuesday. I don't want to withdraw any more money from our savings account if we can avoid it.
B: Agreed. That account is our only real financial asset if the car breaks down or the heating fails.
A: Let's look at the regular payments and see which ones are a true necessity and which ones we can cancel.
B: We're still paying a small fortune for the sports channels, and we hardly ever watch them now.
A: Have we finished the minimum twelve-month contract, or are we still under an obligation to keep paying?
B: The twelve months ended in September, so we can cancel them whenever we like.
A: Good. Cancelling the extra channels is only a short-term fix if food prices keep rising, though.
B: True, but if we cancel those and plan our supermarket trips better, our monthly costs should start to decline.
A: Let's cancel the channels tonight and check our electricity contract before the weekend.

Vocabulary: annually, asset, considerable, decline, exceed, fortune, inflation, necessity, obligation, short-term, withdraw
