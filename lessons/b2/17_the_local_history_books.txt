The Local History Books
A: Where did the library get this wonderful box of old diaries and maps?
B: Judge Morrell, who died last winter, left his entire literary collection to the town.
A: Wait—wasn't his family connected to the first people who lived in this valley?
B: Yes. His grandfather, whose name is on the stone monument in the square, was one of the earliest settlers here.
A: Look at this brown notebook. The dark ink is pale now, but the writing is still clear.
B: As a local historian, I've been hoping to read that diary for years. It reveals how the town was established before the rail line arrived.
A: Listen to this short passage on page ten. It describes a great winter storm from that era.
B: May I see it? I'd love to include an extract from that page in my next history book.
A: Look at the drawing next to the text—someone illustrated the old wooden bridge before it fell down.
B: That picture must have been drawn by Clara Morrell, who later gained fame in London as a popular novelist.
A: Did the family possess all the land along the river back then?
B: Only the northern fields. This map, which shows the farm borders, proves that ownership of the south side belonged to the former governor.
A: It's remarkable how well these pages have retained their colour over two centuries.
B: Once we clean the outside of each book, they'll tell today's inhabitants the true tale of our town's origin.
