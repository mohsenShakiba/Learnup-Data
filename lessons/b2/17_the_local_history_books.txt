The Local History Books
A: Hello, I'm trying to find out when the bridge by the square was built. Do you have any local history books?
B: Yes, several. This one by a local historian is very good. There's a whole chapter with photos from the bridge's construction in 1930.
A: 1930? That's funny, my grandfather always said there was an older bridge there before that.
B: He's right. The 1930 bridge replaced a wooden one that came down in a winter storm. There's a short extract here from a newspaper report at the time.
A: Oh, brilliant! Do you have any pictures of what the wooden one looked like?
B: This book is mainly illustrated with photos from 1930, but we do have an 1880 notebook open in the glass case over here.
A: Really? Whose notebook was it?
B: A former teacher's. He kept notes and drawings of the river in it. It's in remarkable condition for its age, though the ink has gone quite pale.
A: Look at that drawing of the river! The wooden bridge was much further north, wasn't it?
B: That's right, just beside the old rail line, near where the stone monument stands today.
A: I walk past there every morning and had no idea. Am I allowed to take a quick photo of it?
B: Yes, as long as your camera light is off. You can't take the notebook out of the library, I'm afraid, but you're welcome to borrow the history book.
A: Great, I'll take the book to the desk now. My grandfather will be so pleased when I show him that drawing!

Vocabulary: construction, extract, former, historian, illustrate, ink, monument, notebook, rail, remarkable
