More Room for the Rabbit
A: Could we give the rabbit more room outside? The children want to let him run around the garden.
B: I'd like him to have more space, but I don't want them to chase him when it's time to bring him in.
A: No, that would scare the poor creature. We could make a separate area with a low fence.
B: He can dig, though. A fence on its own might not keep him in.
A: That's true. We should find out what he needs before we buy anything. Where could the area go?
B: Perhaps beside that bush. There's some shade there in the afternoon.
A: The roots take up a lot of space. We might harm the bush if we tried to dig holes for a fence there.
B: What about the other side of the path? There's more grass, and it's away from the vegetables.
A: That could work. I'll put the plant food in the shed too. The label says it's harmful to animals.
B: Good. Could we use the wire left over from the vegetable beds?
A: I'm not sure. The holes look large enough for him to get his head trapped. Let's ask at the shop where we bought him before we use it.
B: We can take a photo of it and the space we have. They'll need the measurements too.
A: I'll measure it now. Could you sweep those bits of wire off the path first?
B: Yes. The children were going to come out here in a minute.
A: Let's explain that we're making a plan for the rabbit, but his new space isn't ready yet.
B: I'll tell them. They can help us draw it once we've measured everything.
A: Good. We'll take the drawing to the shop tomorrow and find out what else we need.
B: And until it's ready, we'll keep him in the area he uses now.

Vocabulary: bush, chase, creature, dig, harm, harmful, root, scare, sweep, trap
