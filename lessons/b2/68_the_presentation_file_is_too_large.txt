The Presentation File Is Too Large
A: I can't upload the presentation for tomorrow's client meeting. The file is larger than the company website allows.
B: How many slides have you made?
A: Only twelve, though I inserted the full-size photos from our visit to the factory. There are multiple photos on most pages.
B: That might explain it. Do they really need to stay that big?
A: Probably not. We'll only show them on the meeting room screen. We won't print them as large posters.
B: Try saving a second copy first. Then you can reduce the resolution of the photos without changing your main file.
A: Where do I do that?
B: Select a photo and click this icon. It looks like a little picture with arrows around it.
A: Here? There's an option to change all the photos at once.
B: Yes. Try that on the copy, then check whether the pictures still look clear when you show a whole slide.
A: They look fine. The file is significantly smaller now, though it's still just above the maximum size.
B: Have you got any audio clips in there?
A: No, just an animation on the first slide showing a truck moving across the map.
B: Does it actually need to move?
A: Not really—it just shows the delivery route. I could replace it with a still map.
B: Try that. And if it's still slightly over, you can convert the whole presentation to a PDF.
A: Good idea—the client said either format was acceptable. I'll check every page before I upload it.
B: Just keep your main presentation file too, in case you want to edit anything later.

Vocabulary: animation, audio, convert, icon, insert, multiple, PDF, resolution, significantly, slide
