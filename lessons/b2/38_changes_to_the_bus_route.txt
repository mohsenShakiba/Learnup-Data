Changes to the Bus Route
A: Have you seen the notice on the bus stop about our morning route to the station?
B: No, I've just got here. Are they changing the times again?
A: They want to extend the route so the bus goes through the new suburb on the west side of town.
B: That will add at least fifteen minutes to the duration of our journey every morning.
A: That's why some people on our street oppose the plan. If we reach the station later, we'll miss the eight o'clock train.
B: I can understand why they're doing it, though. The inhabitants of that new district don't have any public transportation yet.
A: True, and the council says the extra passengers will justify running buses every ten minutes instead of every twenty.
B: Higher frequency would be useful in the evenings. The real problem in the morning is the slow drive along the main highway.
A: Exactly. Once the buses join the cars by the bridge, there's always a long delay.
B: Didn't the council talk about painting a bus lane on that stretch of road last year?
A: Yes, and the notice says they're asking for comments on the route before Friday.
B: If they put the bus lane in first, the extra stop in the new suburb wouldn't make us late for the train.
A: Let's both send that suggestion to the council website tonight.
B: I'll do it on my phone while we're on the bus. Oh look, here it comes now.

Vocabulary: delay, district, duration, extend, frequency, highway, inhabitant, justify, lane, oppose, suburb, transportation
