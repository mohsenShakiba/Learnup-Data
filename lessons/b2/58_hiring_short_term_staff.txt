Hiring Short-Term Staff
A: Do you have a minute before you go back to the packing floor? I've just checked next month's orders.
B: Sure. Are they higher than we expected?
A: Much higher. We've had numerous large orders from new shops, and the total already exceeds last December's.
B: They're coming in more rapidly than usual, then. Our current workforce won't manage that on their own, especially with two people away next month.
A: Exactly. Even with extra hours, our weekly output wouldn't be high enough.
B: Could we bring in temporary staff on a short-term contract until the end of the busy period?
A: That's what I was thinking. I called a recruitment agency this morning to see who they have available.
B: Can they send people who have done packing work before?
A: Yes, they have four people with experience.
B: Does our budget permit taking on all four for six weeks?
A: It should, if our manager approves the cost today. Do we have room for four more people on the floor?
B: Yes, if we use the two spare tables at the back. With experience, they should learn how we pack orders in a day or two.
A: Good. What about the new label software we were planning to implement next Monday?
B: Doing both in the same week will slow everyone down. The transition to new software always takes time.
A: Good point. Let's leave that until January. Who can help the new staff if any problems arise while you're checking deliveries?
B: I'll put Sam at the table beside them. He knows the job well.
A: Great. If our manager agrees today, they can start next Monday. I'll take the figures to her office now.
B: Let me know what she says, and I'll get those spare tables ready.

Vocabulary: arise, exceed, implement, numerous, output, permit, rapidly, recruitment, short-term, transition, workforce
