Filming on the Island
A: Look at these pictures from our island trip. You managed to capture the whole animal colony on the rocks!
B: I was lucky with the light. If the morning clouds hadn't cleared by nine, those distant rocks wouldn't have been visible at all.
A: How did you get such a low camera angle near the water without getting wet?
B: I hid behind a large stone near the shore so I could observe the group without disturbing their natural habitat.
A: What about the sea creatures we saw in the rock pools? Did your wide lens work well there?
B: Yes, the water was so shallow and clear that I didn't even need a camera flash to film the fish.
A: I'm still glad we left the dark sea cave before the water came up.
B: Me too! If the conservation guide hadn't called us back, the high waves would have trapped us inside for hours.
A: We almost lost our equipment bag when that big wave hit the small boat, though.
B: True—if you hadn't held the bag in time, it would have floated out into the vast ocean.
A: Did you get any film of the grey sea animals before we had to abandon the beach?
B: Just a short one. Most of them had already dived into the deep water, but one curious animal emerged right beside our boat.
A: It's amazing how this rare species manages to find enough food for its winter survival in such cold water.
B: This wildlife film will be our best work yet once we finish putting the parts together.
