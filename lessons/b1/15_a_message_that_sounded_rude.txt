A Message That Sounded Rude
A: I think I've annoyed Sam. He hasn't replied to my message.
B: When did you send it, and what did you say?
A: Half an hour ago. He invited me to his flat for dinner on Friday, and I wrote, "Great, please don't cook this time!"
B: Oh, no! Why did you write that?
A: Last month he burned our dinner and we ordered pizza instead, so I was trying to be funny. I didn't mean to upset him.
B: Without hearing how you say it, a message like that could sound quite rude. He might take it seriously when he reads it.
A: Now I feel really embarrassed. That was such a careless message to send. I wonder whether he's angry with me.
B: Don't worry yet. It's only been half an hour, so he may just be busy at work.
A: Should I call him to apologize?
B: Some people prefer a phone call, but personally, I think you ought to send a short message first. You don't want to bother him in a meeting.
A: Good point. How about: "Sorry, that was a silly thing to say! I was thinking about the pizza last month. I'd love to come on Friday, and I can bring cake."
B: Yes, that explains what you meant straight away. You can apologize again when you see him.
A: All right, I've sent it.
B: Good. Now give him a little time to reply.

Vocabulary: annoy, apologize, bother, careless, embarrassed, personally, seriously, silly, upset, wonder
