One Comment Too Many
A: Why are you looking so annoyed?
B: I'm really mad about a reply to my comment on the local news website.
A: What did you write in your comment?
B: I just said our town needs more evening buses, and a complete stranger called it a stupid idea.
A: That's unnecessary. People can reject an idea without being so rude.
B: Exactly! Surely people should be able to disagree about local buses in a polite way.
A: I agree, though it looks like you're writing a long reply right now.
B: I am. I typically ignore comments like that, but he's treating me like an enemy.
A: Still, you don't have to turn a comment about buses into a battle.
B: Why should I stay quiet when he wasn't polite to me?
A: Because posting a careless reply while you're angry won't help. I tried your way once, and the only consequence was more angry messages all evening.
B: Did the other person change their mind in the end?
A: Of course not. In my experience, it's very rare for an online argument to end well.
B: Then how do you stop thinking about it?
A: I use a little trick. I write out my reply, and then I wait ten minutes before sending it. By then, I usually decide not to send it.
B: Well, I've just finished writing mine. Let me read it again.
A: And? Do you still want to send it?
B: No, it's longer than the news article itself. I'll close the page and go for a walk instead.

Vocabulary: battle, careless, consequence, enemy, mad, rare, reject, surely, trick, typically
