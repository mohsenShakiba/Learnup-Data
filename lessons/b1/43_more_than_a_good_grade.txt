More Than a Good Grade
A: Did you use much French on your holiday?
B: I tried! I always got good grades at secondary school, and reading was my main strength, but real conversations were much harder.
A: Was understanding people the biggest difficulty?
B: Yes, because native speakers talk fast and don't stop after every sentence like my old teacher did.
A: Did it help when you asked them to speak more slowly?
B: It did, and everyone was very kind. I also used an app on my phone to translate a few words.
A: I can relate to that. I know lots of words, but I sometimes forget them the moment I need them.
B: Exactly. It's easy to read a definition in a dictionary and understand it, but using the word in a conversation is another thing.
A: Did communication get easier by the end of the trip?
B: Much easier, especially once I stopped trying to say everything perfectly.
A: That sounds like real progress.
B: It was. At school, I used to revise for hours before a test, but on holiday, I just wanted to order lunch.
A: And did you get the lunch you wanted?
B: Well, not on the first day. I asked for six sandwiches instead of two by mistake.
A: Six? What did you do with all of them?
B: I shared the extra ones with some people in the park, and we ended up talking all afternoon.
A: So your mistake actually helped you use your French!
B: It did. I made a few mistakes, but I made some new friends too.

Vocabulary: communication, definition, difficulty, grade, native, relate, revise, secondary, strength, translate
