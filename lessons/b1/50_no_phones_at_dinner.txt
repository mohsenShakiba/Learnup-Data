No Phones at Dinner
A: My mum has just announced a new ban on phones at the dinner table.
B: Really? How do you feel about that?
A: I strongly agree with her. Because we spend most of the week apart, it's nice when everyone's involved in the conversation at dinner.
B: That's a new attitude for you! You used to read messages while we ate.
A: I know, until I noticed how annoying it was when my brother did it every evening.
B: What does he think of the new rule?
A: He complained at first because he has this belief that it's rude if you don't answer a message immediately.
B: Surely his friends can wait until dinner is finished, can't they?
A: Exactly. So I thought he was going to be the problem. It didn't occur to me that Mum might be the one to forget her own rule.
B: No way! What happened?
A: In the middle of dinner, the sudden sound of her phone ringing made everyone at the table stop talking.
B: Did she actually answer it?
A: She did! It was my aunt, and Mum said she was only going to talk for a minute.
B: Let me guess, it took longer than a minute?
A: Almost ten minutes. My dad kept giving her a signal to finish the call, but she kept talking.
B: What was so important that it couldn't wait?
A: She was telling my aunt all about our new phone ban.
B: So your aunt heard more of the family conversation than anyone at the table!
A: Yes, and my brother enjoyed watching that much more than checking his messages.
B: I think your mum needs to leave her phone in another room tomorrow night.

Vocabulary: announce, apart, attitude, ban, belief, involved, occur, signal, strongly, sudden
