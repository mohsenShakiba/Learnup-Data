A Wedding for Two Families
A: Congratulations on getting engaged! Have you chosen a ring yet?
B: Thanks! Yes, just a simple one. I told Sam I didn't want a diamond ring because I lose expensive things too easily.
A: It looks lovely on you. Have the two of you started planning the wedding?
B: We're trying to, but both of our families already have their own plans.
A: Do their ideas match yours?
B: Not at all. Ours is a quiet lunch, while theirs is a huge party for everyone they've ever met.
A: Have you chosen a definite date for it yet?
B: Yes, September the tenth. My mum says a wedding is a special occasion, so we need a big celebration.
A: What does Sam think about that?
B: He agrees with me. We want to spend our money on our life together because the marriage matters more than one day.
A: Have you explained that to your families?
B: We have, and right after that, his mum started talking about the bride arriving in a special car.
A: Oh dear! It sounds like they're just really excited for you.
B: I know they are. I just want everyone to understand that our happiness doesn't depend on an expensive party.
A: They probably want to give you a special day that you'll remember forever.
B: That's true. So we've agreed to have lunch with our families first, and then invite friends for cake in the evening.
A: That sounds like a great plan. How many people is that all together?
B: I'm afraid to count. Our small wedding is growing every time we talk about it.

Vocabulary: bride, celebration, definite, diamond, engaged, forever, happiness, marriage, occasion, ours, theirs
