At Home but at Work
A: How's working from home going? Do you like it?
B: Mostly, yes, but my family still thinks being at home means I'm free all day.
A: What kind of things do they ask you to do?
B: Yesterday, my dad called me downstairs to help him with a heavy box. My job is to assist customers online, and I was right in the middle of a call.
A: Was he aware that you were talking to a customer?
B: No. Previously, I only stayed at home on my days off, so he still forgets that I'm working.
A: Have you explained that your working hours haven't changed?
B: Yes, several times, but whenever he needs help, he expects an immediate answer.
A: Can you combine your breaks with helping him?
B: Sometimes I can, but the calls are almost continuous in the morning.
A: Does the management team expect you to stay at your desk all day?
B: Not really. Their primary aim is getting the work done well, so I can choose when I take my breaks.
A: Then tell your dad what time those breaks are. He probably just doesn't understand the importance of leaving you alone during calls.
B: I'll try that tomorrow. I don't want to make a big complaint about it, because he really is trying to support me.
A: Is he? In what way?
B: Well, after yesterday, he made a sign for my door that says, "Working. Please don't enter."
A: That was very kind of him! Has it helped?
B: A little, except that ten minutes later, he knocked to ask if the sign looked straight.

Vocabulary: assist, aware, combine, complaint, continuous, immediate, importance, management, previously, primary
