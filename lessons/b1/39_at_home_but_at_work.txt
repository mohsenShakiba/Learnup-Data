At Home but at Work
A: How's working from home? Do you like it?
B: Mostly. But my family thinks being at home means I'm free all day.
A: What do they ask you to do?
B: Yesterday, my dad wanted help with a heavy box. My job is to assist customers online, and I was in the middle of a call.
A: Was he aware of that?
B: No. Previously, I only stayed home on my days off. He still sees it that way.
A: Have you explained that your hours haven't changed?
B: Yes. But if he needs help, he expects an immediate answer.
A: Can you combine a break with helping him?
B: Sometimes. But the calls are almost continuous in the morning.
A: Does the management team expect you to stay online all day?
B: No. Their primary aim is getting the work done well. I can choose when I take a break.
A: Then tell your dad when those breaks are. He probably doesn't understand the importance of leaving you alone during calls.
B: I'll try that. I don't want this to turn into a complaint about him. He wants to help.
A: Of course. You just need to know when to leave each other alone.
B: Actually, he's already made a sign for my door: "Working. Please don't enter."
A: That was kind. Has it helped?
B: A little. Yesterday, he knocked to ask if the sign looked straight.

Vocabulary: assist, aware, combine, complaint, continuous, immediate, importance, management, previously, primary
