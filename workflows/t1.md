# T1: Natural and Believable Dialogue Test

## Purpose

Assess whether the conversation reads like a believable exchange between real people speaking English in the given situation. The wording, responses, and events should make sense without feeling forced.

This test evaluates naturalness and plausibility only. It has no new-vocabulary minimum and does not assess vocabulary coverage or grammar progression.

## Review Checks

Read the complete conversation, then examine each turn in context.

1. **Clear context:** Can the reader understand who is talking, what is happening, and why they are talking? The opening should provide enough context to follow the exchange without relying on the title or a later explanation. Do not require unnecessary introductions or details.
2. **Believable motivation:** Does each speaker have a reason to say each line at that moment? Flag questions about facts they already know, unnecessary explanations of shared knowledge, and lines that appear to exist only to insert a vocabulary word.
3. **Connected responses:** Does each reply reasonably respond to the previous words, emotions, or actions? Reassurance should address the actual concern. Topic changes, indirect answers, and misunderstandings are acceptable when the context makes them believable.
4. **Consistent logic:** Track what each speaker knows, what has happened, and what remains unresolved. Actions, rules, timing, prices, and arrangements should stay consistent unless the conversation explains a change. Do not invent an unmentioned event to repair a contradiction.
5. **Natural spoken English:** Would these people plausibly use this wording in this situation? Consider their relationship and level of formality. Flag stiff phrasing, unnatural word combinations, and unnecessary repetition. Simple language, contractions, fragments, and short answers are acceptable; slang and fillers are not required.
6. **Believable progression and ending:** Does the exchange develop for a reason and stop at a plausible point? Flag abrupt resolutions, unexplained changes of mind, and forced jokes or moral lessons. A practical next step or ordinary goodbye is sufficient; not every problem must be resolved.

## Review Result

- Give a verdict: **Pass** or **Needs revision**. Pass when no concrete naturalness or plausibility issue is found; do not require stylistic perfection.
- For each issue, quote the exact line or lines, explain why they do not fit the surrounding exchange, and suggest the smallest change that addresses the problem.
- Distinguish actual problems from optional style preferences. Do not fail a conversation merely because another phrasing is possible.
- If a judgment depends on missing context, state the uncertainty rather than inventing facts or presenting a preference as an error.
- Check suggested fixes against the whole conversation so they do not create new inconsistencies. If the conversation passes, give a brief reason without forcing a rewrite.
